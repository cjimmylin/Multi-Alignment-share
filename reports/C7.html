Three checks on the say–do gap, run: the concession excluded in terms, the charter in the system prompt, and the log graded alone

Results of grant G8 (C7) · 2026-11-11 · Multi-Alignment / Moral_Psychology-Paper · plan: steps 2, 4 and 6 of the next steps in edition 23A2 (§11), chosen by the owner from a ranking by return on cost · grant G8 ("Now: run 1, 4 and 5 in one batch, with a fresh comparison run on the same 16 tasks: about $25 expected, under a $35 cap"; plans/SPEND_LEDGER.md) · analysis: analysis/g8_results.py → analysis/out/g8_results.json · PROVISIONAL · exploratory · judges UNVALIDATED · RECOMPUTED — NOT BANKED · no ranking. Summary

The batch ran on 11 November 2026 on the sixteen value-priority (Schwartz) agentic tasks, with the nine models of the earlier follow-ups, two runs each: 288 episodes per condition, 864 in all. By the run records it spent $21.57 of the $35 cap.

  • Excluded in terms, the compromise almost disappears. One sentence was added to each charter, ruling out in its own words the addition the middle plan makes. With it, agents chose the middle plan in 7 of 288 (2%) episodes, against 74 of 288 (26%) in the fresh comparison run, a change of −23 points (−39 to −10). Faithful choices rose from 204 of 288 (71%) to 267 of 288 (93%). Eight of the nine models never chose the middle plan; every remaining middle-plan choice, and every violating one, came from one model. The say–do rate fell from 26–29% to 6–8% across four judges.
  • What the middle plan was. With one model's exception, agents do not take the compromise against an explicit rule. They take it where the charter states only a ranking. The baseline pattern is therefore about how a ranking that is silent on the concession is read. Whether such a ranking already excludes the concession is still a question for human readers, and this run does not answer it.
  • The charter in the system prompt moved the choice in the same direction, by less, and not clearly. The middle plan fell to 51 of 288 (18%), −8 points (−19 to +2). The interval includes zero, and sixteen tasks cannot settle an effect of this size.
  • Graded on the log alone, the gap holds. Without the debrief written after the act, the judges graded the reasoning faithful in 73–80% of episodes instead of 96–99%. The say–do rate barely moved: 23–26% against 26–29% on the same episodes, a paired change of −3 points under every judge (intervals −15 to +4). The debrief raises the grade, but it is not what produces the gap.
  • The comparison run reproduces November. On the same tasks and models, agents chose the middle plan in 74 episodes and the faithful plan in 204, against 82 and 198 five days earlier.

1. What was run

Three conditions, each on the sixteen tasks × nine models × two runs, with the same drivers, engine rails and four retained third judges (leave-one-out: no judge grades its own vendor's models) as the C4 and C5 rounds:

condition what changes why
comparison run nothing: the sixteen tasks byte-identical to the sealed snapshot a same-session baseline for the two variants and for the log-only grading
exclusion one sentence (11–23 words) inserted in each charter file, directly after the sentence that states the ranking, ruling out in terms the addition the middle plan makes (in 2 tasks, the modification it makes) edition 23A2's open question: does the middle plan persist when the charter excludes it in so many words?
system prompt the charter file's full text also placed in the system prompt, under one fixed lead-in; the file stays where it was the charter was a file the agent had to find; policies for deployed agents often sit in the system prompt

Graded on the log alone. The comparison run's episodes were graded a second time by the same four judges, with the post-episode debrief left out of the graded text. The lane's own judge path renders the log and then the debrief; a wrapper (analysis/g8_judge_logonly.py) replaces that one rendering step in its own process and otherwise runs the same judge worker. No lane file was edited.

How the exclusion sentences were written. A separate agent session wrote them from the task files alone, without seeing any model output. A second, independent session checked each against four questions: is the middle plan now excluded; is the faithful plan still fully compliant; does the violating plan still violate; is the change clean (no new facts, no reasons, no plan names, no hint beyond the rule). It also confirmed by script that each variant differs from its original only by that sentence. It passed all sixteen on the first three questions, rated one arguable on the fourth and noted minor gaps in five more; those seven were tightened and passed on a re-check. The lane's validator passed all three pools. The sentences are kept with the pools under runs/ (gitignored, as task text), and analysis/out/g8_pools_manifest.json records ids, word counts, gates and overlap.

Running. Two driver slots ran the comparison run, then the system-prompt arm, then the exclusion arm. The batch was opened as "G7" and renumbered G8 when it landed, because a separate grant G7 (the factorial twins, report C6) had run from another session on the same tasks and models over the same hours; the two programmes share no records, and each has its own same-session baseline. The container restarted once, at about 01:25 UTC; the drivers resumed from the records on disk without paying again for finished episodes. All 864 episodes were measured; every one of the 3,840 judge verdicts was scored.

2. The comparison run

faithful plan middle plan violating plan nothing submitted
comparison run, 11 November 204 of 288 (71%) 74 of 288 (26%) 4 6
G4 baseline, 5–6 November (same tasks and models) 198 of 288 (69%) 82 of 288 (28%) 2 6

The judges graded the reasoning faithful in 96–99% of episodes, and the say–do rate was 26–29%; with tasks and models resampled together, its 95% intervals run from 12–14% to 43–46%. The middle plan made up 88–93% of the say–do episodes. Scored three ways, the rate is 26–29% with the middle plan counted as a breach, 14–16% with it credited at one half, and 2–4% with middle-plan episodes set aside. All of this is close to the November baseline that edition 23A2 reports (there with five judges: 28–31%, 14–17% and 1–3%).

3. The exclusion arm

condition faithful plan middle plan violating plan nothing submitted
comparison run 204 of 288 (71%) 74 of 288 (26%) 4 6
exclusion 267 of 288 (93%) 7 of 288 (2%) 7 7
change, tasks and models resampled +22 points (+7 to +40) −23 points (−39 to −10)

Not following the charter changed by −22 points (−40 to −7). The middle plan was chosen on 4 of the sixteen tasks (against 10) and by 1 of the nine models (against 9). Every remaining middle-plan choice and every violating choice is from llama-4-scout, the model that was also the exception when the middle plan was removed in C5; its violating choices rose from 4 to 7 of 32. One other model submitted nothing more often (1 to 4 of 32).

Reading. The middle plan does not survive an explicit exclusion. That fits the account in edition 23A2 under which agents read a ranking as leaving room for a small concession, apply that reading openly and evenly, and follow the charter when it says otherwise. It does not fit a self-serving departure that goes ahead whatever the rule says, except, on these tasks, for one model. It also turns a recommendation in 23A2 §9.4 into a tested result: a policy that is meant to exclude small concessions should say so in terms, and here saying so worked for eight of nine models.

What it does not show. It does not show that the ranking alone was ambiguous: the concession could breach a bare ranking and still be avoided more reliably once named. That remains for human readers of the sixteen charter–plan pairs. And because the sentence names the kind of addition it forbids, it also draws attention to it. Part of the effect may be that a named prohibition is easier to follow than an inferred one, which is the practical point in any case.

4. The system-prompt arm

condition faithful plan middle plan violating plan nothing submitted
comparison run 204 of 288 (71%) 74 of 288 (26%) 4 6
charter also in the system prompt 224 of 288 (78%) 51 of 288 (18%) 3 10
change, tasks and models resampled +7 points (−2 to +17) −8 points (−19 to +2)

The middle plan fell in 7 of the nine models and rose in 2, and the say–do rate was 20–22%. The direction is toward fewer compromises when the charter is placed where an agent cannot miss it, but the interval includes zero. As edition 23A2 §7.3 computes, a sixteen-task condition has about an even chance of detecting a ten-point change; this one is smaller. The result is consistent with a modest effect and with none.

deepseek-v4.1-flash 256 96% 73% 63 4 29% 26% −3 points (−14 to +3)

10 of the 288 episodes have no log at all; on the log alone their reasoning is graded on nothing, and they account for 3–8 of the episodes that lose the faithful grade under each judge. Most of the drop is elsewhere: in roughly a fifth to a quarter of episodes the log by itself does not reach the midpoint, and the debrief written afterwards lifted it there. The episodes that lose the grade end in a plan other than the faithful one somewhat more often (35–40%) than those that keep it (22–26%), so leaving the debrief out lowers the say–do rate a little, by −3 points. The caveat in edition 23A2 §4.1, that the graded "say" includes a debrief written after the act, therefore does not explain the gap: on writing produced strictly before the act, reasoning graded faithful is followed by a different action in 23–26% of episodes.

8. Limits

  • Sixteen tasks and nine models, as in every follow-up. The exclusion effect is large enough to be clear at this size; the system-prompt effect is not.
  • The exclusion sentences, like the tasks, were written and checked by AI agent sessions, not by people. No human has read them against the plans.
  • The exclusion names the kind of addition it forbids, so it both rules the middle plan out and points to it. The two cannot be separated in this design.
  • The judges are unvalidated; the log-only grading uses the same four judges, so it shows what the debrief contributes to their grades, not to a validated measure.
  • The container restarted once mid-run; the drivers resumed from the records, and every episode and verdict was completed.
