One list for both lines: the say–do and moral-hypocrisy workstreams, merged and force-ranked by return

Combined workstream ranking (C3) · 2026-11-05 · Multi-Alignment / Moral_Psychology-Paper · source: the say–do workstream proposal (A3, say-do/reports/A3_say-do_workstreams_roi.md) and the moral-hypocrisy workstream proposal (B3, moral-hypocrisy/reports/B3_moral-hypocrisy_workstreams_roi.md), whose impact ratings, person-days and cash estimates are used as given; scoring by analysis/c3_rank_workstreams.py, output analysis/out/c3_workstream_rank.json · planning arithmetic, not a measurement: no model is called and no model is ranked · every paid step still needs its own grant, probe and stopping rule under lane law · PROVISIONAL · judges UNVALIDATED · no ranking.

Summary

A3 proposed eighteen workstreams for the say–do line and B3 sixteen for the moral-hypocrisy line. After merging the six workstreams that both describe, splitting two that bundle separable tests, and setting aside one conditional item and one that B3 folds into another, the two lists give 29 distinct workstreams. They were scored by two different rules: A3 counted $25 of spend as one person-day and B3 counted $100. This report puts every workstream on one rule (impact × breadth × 10 ÷ cost units, with $50 as one person-day and a 1.25 credit for work both papers need) and force-ranks all 29 with no ties. It then checks the order under eighteen variants of the rule's three arbitrary choices.

The recommendation is the ten best-ranked workstreams whose impact is at least 3 (Table 1). Together they cost about 24.5 person-days and $68 in expected metered spend (cap $88), and they fit in four calendar weeks with three small grants. Eight of the ten stay in the top ten in at least fourteen of the eighteen scoring variants. The last two places are contested, and the contest turns on one choice. The disclosure arm holds its place whenever impact counts linearly and loses it whenever decisive work is weighted more, when the option-set manipulation takes it.

Three findings about the combined list matter more than any single rank:

  • The priority-reversed twins come first among the substantive workstreams, and they are a gate. If models take the middle plan at the same rate whichever way the charter states the priority, then the charter plays no role. Both framings would then lose their premise: the say–do paper's "assigned priority" and the hypocrisy paper's "charter–action gap". Every charter-dependent arm should wait on that result, which costs about $8 and three person-days.
  • Cheap analyses of data already collected dominate the top of the list. Three of the ten cost a dollar or less, none costs more than $16, and none takes more than four person-days. The disclosure audit is the most valuable of them, because it decides the vocabulary of both papers.
  • The four highest-impact metered arms rank in the middle, not the top. These are the routing arm, the option-set manipulation, the single-factor pressure twins and the expert read. Each is rated 5 for impact, but each costs six to ten person-days, or carries honoraria. They are the next tranche, not the first, with one exception argued in §5: the expert read's recruitment should start now because its lead time is longer than the whole first tranche.

1. How the two proposals were put on one scale

Impact. Both proposals rate impact from 1 to 5 with anchors that line up. In A3, 5 means the work decides a mechanism question the paper leaves open or turns an argued-around threat into evidence. In B3, 5 means it decides whether the word "hypocrisy" may be used, or supplies the validity a psychology venue requires. At 4, A3 closes a named reviewer objection or reaches a new population, and B3 separates two rival readings or answers a council MAJOR. At 3, both strengthen a claim already made. At 2, both mean disclosure or robustness, and at 1 both mean cosmetic. Each paper's 5 is therefore "decides a central open question of that paper". A workstream keeps its source rating, and a merged one takes the higher of its two ratings.

Breadth. Six workstreams are proposed by both A3 and B3. Run once, each serves both papers, so it carries a breadth credit of 1.25. Breadth is credited only where both proposals actually asked for the work. A workstream that one proposal asked for and the other paper might also use gets no credit, because that would be this report's judgment rather than the proposals'.

Cost. Cost units are person-days plus cash divided by $50. The rate sits between A3's $25 and B3's $100. Person-days are the lane's own time, as both proposals counted it. Cash is the expected figure, while the cap governs grants. Honoraria and participant payments count as cash at the same rate as metered spend, since the lane has one budget.

Return and the force rank. ROI = impact × breadth × 10 ÷ cost units. Rows are ordered by ROI rounded to two decimals. A tie goes to the higher impact, then the lower cash, then the row that more workstreams depend on, so the order is strict. A3's own caution still holds: two rows within a factor of 1.5 of each other differ by less than the precision of their estimates, and §4 orders them by sequencing.

Robustness. The rule has three arbitrary choices, so the ranking was repeated under 18 variants:

  • the dollar rate at $25, $50 and $100;
  • impact linear, or convex with 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8, which rewards decisive work more;
  • the breadth credit at 1.0, 1.25 and 1.5.

Table 3 gives each row's best and worst rank across the variants, and how many of the 18 put it in the top ten.

Merging. When both proposals described the same work, the merged row takes the larger effort and cash estimate for the shared part, plus the increment for whatever only one proposal included. Table 2 records each merge. Two of B3's items were split or folded. Its menu manipulation (W7) shares two variants with A3's option-set arm, and its third variant, the hidden-consequences menu, is a separate test of a separate prediction, so it is ranked alone. Its cross-cultural quota (W15) is folded into the observer study's cap, as B3 recommends. A3's charter-placement arm (W7b) stays conditional on the charter-form table and is not ranked, as in A3.

3. What the ranking says

The top is cheap and analytic, and it is stable. The first eleven rows cost between $0 and $16 each and one to four person-days. Eight of the recommended ten are in the top ten in at least fourteen of the eighteen variants, and the reversed twins never fall below fourth. Neither proposal's own rule would change that: at $25 per person-day and at $100, the same eight lead. The ranking is not sensitive to the choice of rate because, for these workstreams, person-days and not dollars dominate the cost.

The tenth place is genuinely contested. The disclosure arm (rank 9) and the salience arms (rank 11, tenth among eligible rows) hold the last two places under the central rule. But they are in the top ten in only 9 and 8 of the 18 variants, and the split is clean. The disclosure arm is in the top ten in all nine variants that count impact linearly and in none that weight it convexly. The option-set manipulation (rank 15, impact 5, serves both papers) is the mirror image: it is in the top ten in six convex variants and in no linear one. The analysis ablation (rank 12) enters only at the $100 rate with convex impact. So the tenth place is a choice about values, not arithmetic. If the lane would rather buy one decisive experiment than two cheaper partial ones, the option-set manipulation replaces the disclosure arm. That swap costs four more person-days and $16 more expected spend. The disclosure arm also depends on the salience result, as B3 notes, so it is the less independent of the two.

The high-impact arms are the second tranche. The routing arm (rank 20), the option-set manipulation (15), the single-factor pressure twins (23) and the cost gradient (24) each answer a mechanism question, but each takes six to nine person-days of authoring, driving and analysis. Under convex impact the option-set manipulation and the routing arm rise to ranks 7 and 12 at best. The pressure twins rise to 13 at best. None reaches the top ten under the central rule. They follow naturally once the reversed twins have shown that the charter is doing work.

Human work ranks last on return, and that is the wrong signal to act on. The expert read (rank 27), the human comparison arm (28) and the observer study (29) rank last because their cash dwarfs every metered arm. B3 prices the observer study at about $2,500 against $8 for a sixteen-item arm. Their value is not in doubt, however. The expert read is the only route to a validity statement about the judged channel in either paper. The observer study is the only way to measure, rather than assert, that people would call the gap hypocrisy. Both are bottlenecked by calendar time (recruitment, ethics review), not by lane days. §5 treats them as parallel tracks to open now.

Shared work is rare at the top, and that is useful to know. Three of the six workstreams both proposals asked for are in the recommended ten: the reversed twins, the disclosure audit and the structured intended-action field. The other three shared ones (the option-set manipulation, the pressure twins and the expert read) are expensive. The two papers need different evidence beyond the shared core, so the plan should not assume one programme serves both papers for free.

5. Outside the ten: what to start in parallel, and what comes next

Start now, in parallel: the expert read's recruitment. It ranks 27th on return, but its lane cost is five person-days. Its calendar cost is three to four weeks of recruitment, which is as long as the whole first tranche. A3 puts it in its first tranche, and B3 says to start it now, for that reason. The packet is drawn and sealed, the protocol is fixed and the ethics are prepared. Opening recruitment in week 1 does not take a slot from the ten. The A-26 STOP-and-ask applies before any of its numbers is used.

Open the ethics application for the observer study if the hypocrisy paper is going to a psychology venue. If B2's framing survives the disclosure audit and the reversed twins, a psychology venue will ask for the observer study. Its ethics review is eight to twelve weeks long, so the application is the one human-study step worth starting before the evidence is in. If the paper is aimed at a machine-learning venue, defer it.

The second tranche, after week 4. The next eight workstreams of impact 4 or 5, in rank order (ranks 12 to 23):

  1. Analysis ablation crossed with observation (rank 12): the cheapest test of licensing against the mirror.
  2. Option-set manipulation (rank 15): the within-instrument test of the option-set claim both papers make.
  3. Post-capitulation re-elicitation (rank 16): turns the sycophancy result into a belief test.
  4. Release package (rank 18): opens the datasets-and-benchmarks venue.
  5. Registered report with Hua (rank 19): needs the hidden-consequences menu and the base-rate arm first.
  6. Routing arm (rank 20): decides between a priority stated and not enacted and one that never entered the decision.
  7. Base-rate arm (rank 21): puts human and model rates on one option set.
  8. Single-factor pressure twins (rank 23): un-bundles the pressure. B3's suggestion to merge them with the cost gradient into one factorial authoring campaign of about 112 twins is worth taking if both are wanted.

With the full second tranche, the lane would have a mechanism claim (routing), an option-set claim (manipulation) and a decomposed pressure claim (single-factor twins), which A3's rubric counts as main-track material.

Later or conditional. The reasoning-effort arm, the evaluation-awareness covariate, the owner-machine repeats, the related-work section (needed before any submission but not urgent), the cross-benchmark concordance, the organizational-decoupling paper and the human comparison arm follow when a venue or a reviewer asks. The charter-placement arm runs only if the charter-form table finds the charters placed uniformly.

6. What the ten would let the papers say

With the ten landed, the say–do paper (A2) and the hypocrisy paper (B2) can each replace hedged sentences with results:

  • The charter's role is measured. The reversed twins measure it rather than assume it. A null retires both framings early and cheaply. A clear result supports both papers' premise at once.
  • The vocabulary is settled by data. The disclosure audit settles it in both papers. A mostly-disclosed split keeps motive-laden words out for good, and B2's "structural hypocrisy" reading weakens, because Jordan et al.'s honest hypocrite is not penalised. A mostly-silent split supports B2's false-signal condition and A2's monitoring implication, with a count beside each.
  • The "say" channel's limits are quantified. The judge-ceiling table, the fifth judge across three schools, the structured field and the sensitivity table say how much of the gap depends on how "say" is graded and on how the middle plan is scored.
  • Batson's components become tests. The salience arms (mirror or labelled coin), the post-action re-description (the missing third component) and the hidden-consequences menu (the wiggle-room test of Hua's prediction) turn B2's mapping onto Batson's taxonomy from analogy into tested components.
  • Self-knowledge of the compromise is measured. The self-prediction probe says whether models can foresee their own compromise, a question A2 currently leaves open.

None of the ten ranks a model or measures a value profile. Each keeps the competence framing and every judged number under unvalidated judges, as A3 and B3 require.

7. Caveats

  • The inputs are estimates. The impact ratings, person-days and cash come from two proposals written in parallel by separate authors from the same record. Their calibration may differ: B3 rates the decisive human work 5 more readily than A3 rates its human arm. The robustness grid covers the rule's choices, not the ratings themselves.
  • ROI is a ranking device, not a measurement. Differences under a factor of 1.5 are within the precision of the estimates. The two contested places are named for that reason.
  • Merged and split rows carry this report's arithmetic. Their figures are documented in Table 2: the reversed twins as one of A3's three twin sets, the hidden-consequences menu as one of B3's three menu variants, and the expert read's cap including A3's third reader.
  • The recommendation applies one rule beyond the arithmetic. An item rated below 3 rides along instead of taking a slot. With the charter-form table counted, the force rank's top ten would drop the salience arms.
  • Nothing here is a grant. Every metered step needs its own grant, probe and stopping rule. The expected spend of the ten, about $68, is a planning figure against a programme that has spent about $220 on metered calls so far: about $28 for the repeat arm and $190 for the four further arms, per article v2's scale and cost paragraph.
