References: Moral Hypocrisy Background

Annotated bibliography for SYNTHESIS.md and BENCHMARK_IMPLICATIONS.md. Every citation key used in those files resolves here. No source appears here that is not in the five research notes.

Conventions. Each entry carries: full citation · citation key · DOI or URL · access and verification status · type · integrity status where relevant · instrument-provenance tag for CS sources (theory-grounded | psych-scale | crowdsourced-folk | ad hoc | n/a) · a 2–4 sentence annotation saying what the source showed and why it matters for the benchmark paper.

Note sigla. N1 = 01_origins_batson_programme.md; N2 = 02_later_paradigms.md; N3 = 03_replication_current_standing.md; N4 = 04_definitions_philosophy.md; N5 = 05_ai_language_models.md (all in research_notes/Moral hypocrisy research background/, compiled 2026-11-04).

Access vocabulary, recorded exactly as the notes give it and never upgraded:

  • full text read: the article, an author manuscript or a working paper was opened (N2 [PRIMARY-FULL]; N4 [FT]; N5 [html]; N3 "opened" for a PDF or article page).
  • abstract: only the abstract or registry metadata was read (N2 [PRIMARY-ABSTRACT]; N4 [ABS]; N5 [abs]; N3 "metadata"). "Metadata only" means no abstract text either.
  • secondary source named: the claim comes through a named source that was read (N2 [SECONDARY]; N4 [SEC]).
  • search snippet: seen only in a search-engine summary (N3 "search-only"; N4 [SNIP]; N5 [search-only]).

Where notes record different access for one source, both are given and the stronger is used, as listed in SYNTHESIS.md §11. Integrity checks were made against Crossref "updated-by" records and named notices on 2026-11-04.

1. Precursors

hartshorne1928deceit

  • Citation: Hartshorne, H., & May, M. A. Studies in the Nature of Character, Vol. 1: Studies in Deceit (Book 1, General methods and results; Book 2, Statistical methods and results). Macmillan, conventionally 1928; APA PsycBooks record dated 1930.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1037/13386-000 · https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037/13386-000
  • Access: metadata only (Crossref record); content not opened [UNVERIFIED-primary, N1]. Date discrepancy (1928 vs 1930) unresolved.
  • Type: book (empirical monograph)
  • Annotation: The conventional citation for the situational inconsistency of honest conduct, and the moral psychologist reviewer's preferred "oldest finding" for the say–do gap. The notes cannot confirm its content or whether Batson's articles cite it; only Batson's 2016 book is (secondarily) shown to discuss it. If the paper cites it, the claim should be limited to the conventional characterization and the date reported as 1928/1930.

blasi1980bridging

  • Citation: Blasi, A. (1980). Bridging moral cognition and moral action: A critical review of the literature. Psychological Bulletin, 88(1), 1–45.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1037/0033-2909.88.1.1 · https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037/0033-2909.88.1.1
  • Access: metadata only (Crossref); content via a search summary only (judgment "often does not result in moral action"; moral identity and self-consistency proposed as the link) [N1]. Whether Batson cites it is unverified.
  • Type: review article
  • Annotation: The standard citation for the judgment–action relation, already cited in PAPER_v22.md §2. The moral psychologist reviewer states that Blasi found a positive, moderate relation; the notes cannot check this. The paper's current wording ("longest-studied and most moderate findings") should not be strengthened beyond what the reviewer and the paper's own verified reference support.

aronson1991condoms

  • Citation: Aronson, E., Fried, C., & Stone, J. (1991). Overcoming denial and increasing the intention to use condoms through the induction of hypocrisy. American Journal of Public Health, 81(12), 1636–1638.
  • DOI / URL: 10.2105/ajph.81.12.1636 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1746661/
  • Access: abstract (PubMed, verbatim) [N1]
  • Type: empirical article
  • Annotation: Induced hypocrisy by making participants mindful of past failures to use condoms and then having them advocate condom use; the induction "decreased denial and led to greater intent to improve condom use". It shows the pre-Batson meaning of hypocrisy (not practising what one preaches) used as an intervention. For the paper it marks the opposite causal direction: awareness of the gap is used to close it, which is what a salience manipulation would test in models.

stone1994inducing

  • Citation: Stone, J., Aronson, E., Crain, A. L., Winslow, M. P., & Fried, C. B. (1994). Inducing hypocrisy as a means of encouraging young adults to use condoms. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 20(1), 116–128.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1177/0146167294201012 · https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0146167294201012
  • Access: abstract (Crossref, verbatim excerpt); "more than twice as likely" figure via secondary source named (Monin & Merritt, pp. 4–5) [N1]
  • Type: empirical article
  • Annotation: Public advocacy plus reminders of past failures led more participants to buy condoms, and to buy more of them, than controls. It is the behavioural demonstration of hypocrisy induction. It supports the claim that making a word–deed gap salient can change action, which is the human precedent for a disclosure or self-comparison item.

stone1997exemplification

  • Citation: Stone, J., Wiegand, A. W., Cooper, J., & Aronson, E. (1997). When exemplification fails: Hypocrisy and the motive for self-integrity. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 72(1), 54–65.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1037//0022-3514.72.1.54 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9008374/
  • Access: abstract (PubMed) [N1]
  • Type: empirical article
  • Annotation: After hypocrisy-aroused dissonance, "significantly more participants chose to reduce dissonance directly", by changing behaviour, rather than by self-affirmation. It appeared in the same JPSP volume as Batson et al. (1997) and uses "self-integrity" in the Aronson sense. It is useful only to show that the two traditions used the word differently at the same time.

stonefernandez2008

  • Citation: Stone, J., & Fernandez, N. C. (2008). To practice what we preach: The use of hypocrisy and cognitive dissonance to motivate behavior change. Social and Personality Psychology Compass, 2(2), 1024–1051.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1111/j.1751-9004.2008.00088.x · https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Stone%20Fernandez%202008%20hypocrisy
  • Access: metadata only (Crossref) [N1]
  • Type: review article
  • Annotation: Review of the hypocrisy-induction programme, cited by Monin and Merritt for the "not practicing what you preach" definition. Its content was not read. Cite only as the review of the induction line, not for any finding.

moninmerritt2012

  • Citation: Monin, B., & Merritt, A. (2012). Moral hypocrisy, moral inconsistency, and the struggle for moral integrity. In M. Mikulincer & P. R. Shaver (Eds.), The Social Psychology of Morality (Herzliya Series, Vol. 3). APA. (Author preprint dated "2010"; published volume year not verified.)
  • DOI / URL: https://gsb-faculty.stanford.edu/benoit-monin/files/2022/04/monin_merritt_hypocrisy_chapter.pdf
  • Access: full text read (author preprint) [N1]. N3 and N4 record the chapter as not opened (search snippet; the "false appearance of virtue" wording, p. 171, via O'Connor et al. 2020). N1's reading is used.
  • Type: book chapter (review)
  • Annotation: The secondary source for Batson's verbatim 1997 definition (p. 3) and for the history from dissonance research to Batson. It lists the three working definitions of hypocrisy in social psychology and proposes broadening the construct to "any claim of morality made to satisfy ulterior (non-moral), self-serving motives". For the paper it is the cleanest source for distinguishing behavioural inconsistency from Batson's motive construct.

2. Batson's programme

batson1997voice

  • Citation: Batson, C. D., Kobrynowicz, D., Dinnerstein, J. L., Kampf, H. C., & Wilson, A. D. (1997). In a very different voice: Unmasking moral hypocrisy. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 72(6), 1335–1348.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1037/0022-3514.72.6.1335 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9177020/ · https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1037/0022-3514.72.6.1335
  • Access: abstract (PubMed, verbatim, N1; OpenAlex, N3 and N4). Definition sentence (p. 1335) via secondary source named (Monin & Merritt, p. 3, with interpolation "[being]"). Cell numbers and self-ratings via secondary source named (Miller, SEP §3, citing pp. 1339–1343). Article body not opened.
  • Type: empirical article (3 studies, 80 female undergraduates)
  • Integrity: no issue recorded.
  • Annotation: Founding paper: in Study 2, 10 of 20 participants flipped a coin and 9 of the 10 assigned themselves the positive task, while flippers rated their action 7.30 against 4.00 for non-flippers who did the same. It defines moral hypocrisy as a motive "to appear moral yet still benefit oneself". This is the source of the reviewers' self-interest objection, and the paper may cite it only for the motive construct it disclaims and for the behavioural pattern, never for a 90 % figure without the n = 10 caveat.

batson1999appearing

  • Citation: Batson, C. D., Thompson, E. R., Seuferling, G., Whitney, H., & Strongman, J. A. (1999). Moral hypocrisy: Appearing moral to oneself without being so. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 77(3), 525–537.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1037/0022-3514.77.3.525 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10510506/
  • Access: abstract (PubMed, N1; OpenAlex, N3 and N4). Cell numbers via secondary source named (SEP, citing pp. 527–530). Study 3 cell numbers not in any source opened.
  • Type: empirical article (3 studies)
  • Annotation: A labelled coin ("SELF to POS" / "OTHER to POS") did not produce fair outcomes (24 of 28 flippers self-assigned), but a mirror did (flippers 85 % without vs 50 % facing it), and only when standards were salient beforehand. It supports Batson's mechanism: avoiding the comparison of behaviour with standard, not misperceiving the behaviour. For the paper it is the key disanalogy: the model's standard is maximally salient, so the Batsonian route is closed and the gap must have another source; it also motivates a salience or self-comparison item.

batsonthompson2001

  • Citation: Batson, C. D., & Thompson, E. R. (2001). Why don't moral people act morally? Motivational considerations. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 10(2), 54–57.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1111/1467-8721.00114 · https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/1467-8721.00114 · https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/1467-8721.00114/
  • Access: abstract (Crossref, verbatim, N1; OpenAlex, N3). Experimenter-flip and shock-variant numbers via secondary source named (SEP, citing pp. 55–56); base of the 91 % ambiguous.
  • Type: review article
  • Annotation: States the three-way taxonomy: moral integrity, moral hypocrisy ("only to appear moral while, if possible, avoiding the cost of being moral") and overpowered integrity (intending to be moral "only to surrender this goal when the costs of being moral become clear"). In a high-cost shock variant half of participants took the better task while admitting it was not right. For the paper it supplies Batson's own non-hypocritical category for a gap that appears with cost and vanishes without it, which fits the pressure-removed twin result.

batson2002alternatives

  • Citation: Batson, C. D., Thompson, E. R., & Chen, H. (2002). Moral hypocrisy: Addressing some alternatives. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 83(2), 330–339.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1037/0022-3514.83.2.330 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12150231/
  • Access: abstract (PubMed, verbatim, N1; OpenAlex, N3). Covert-observation cells and the p. 337 quotation via secondary source named (SEP). Coding of "fiddling" unverified.
  • Type: empirical article (2 studies)
  • Annotation: Ruled out low salience of social standards and "honest winners" as explanations; covert observation found 16 of 32 flippers fiddling, with a morality rating of 5.56 against 3.89 for non-flippers who self-assigned. Its "sham reference to use of the coin" conclusion is the human model of claiming moderate morality for a partial violation. For the paper it is the closest Batson analogue of a middle plan that keeps an appearance of compliance.

batson2003imagine

  • Citation: Batson, C. D., Lishner, D. A., Carpenter, A., Dulin, L., Harjusola-Webb, S., Stocks, E. L., Gale, S., Hassan, O., & Sampat, B. (2003). "... As you would have them do unto you": Does imagining yourself in the other's place stimulate moral action? Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 29(9), 1190–1201.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1177/0146167203254600 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15189613/
  • Access: abstract (PubMed) [N1]; 38 % vs 83 % via secondary source named (SEP, pp. 1198–1199). Venue is PSPB, not JPSP (correction in N1).
  • Type: empirical article (2 experiments)
  • Annotation: Imagine-self perspective-taking did little in a clear-advantage task but raised switches to an equal split from 38 % to 83 % when the moral option was a clear equal split; imagine-other raised empathy and direct help. It shows that a perspective manipulation can close the gap only under specific item structures. For the paper it suggests that the effect of any debiasing instruction will depend on whether the item's charter-coherent action is unambiguous.

batson2006business

  • Citation: Batson, C. D., Collins, E., & Powell, A. A. (2006). Doing business after the fall: The virtue of moral hypocrisy. Journal of Business Ethics, 66(4), 321–335.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1007/s10551-006-0011-8 · https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s10551-006-0011-8 · https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:kap:jbuset:v:66:y:2006:i:4:p:321-335
  • Access: metadata (Crossref); content via secondary source named (search-engine summary of the Springer abstract on the RePEc record) [N1]. Experimental details not verified.
  • Type: conceptual and empirical article
  • Annotation: Argues that features of modern business settings encourage moral hypocrisy, which may be becoming a prescriptive norm. It is Batson's own move from individuals toward organizational settings. Cite only for that argument, flagged as secondary.

batson2008masquerades

  • Citation: Batson, C. D. (2008). Moral masquerades: Experimental exploration of the nature of moral motivation. Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences, 7(1), 51–66 (online 14 Sep 2007).
  • DOI / URL: 10.1007/s11097-007-9058-y · https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s11097-007-9058-y · https://philpapers.org/rec/BATMME-3
  • Access: metadata (Crossref; OpenAlex has no abstract, N3); abstract wording via search snippet only [N1, N4]; the p. 57 argument via secondary source named (SEP).
  • Type: review article
  • Annotation: Reported to state that fully reaching the goal of hypocrisy requires self-deception and that moral integrity should not be equated with altruism; Miller cites it for the argument that denial of responsibility, ability or consequences does not explain the data. Its wording is snippet-only. Do not quote it; quote the 2001 abstract or the 2016 book (via NDPR) instead.

batson2011altruism

  • Citation: Batson, C. D. (2011). Altruism in Humans. Oxford University Press.
  • DOI / URL: cited via https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-moral-psych/
  • Access: secondary source named (SEP bibliography, pp. 222–224 on moral hypocrisy); not opened [N1].
  • Type: book
  • Annotation: Cited by Miller for Batson's characterization of moral hypocrisy. Listed only because SEP's account of the three hypocrisy components draws on it. Not a citation the paper needs.

batson2016wrong

  • Citation: Batson, C. D. (2016). What's Wrong with Morality? A Social-Psychological Perspective. Oxford University Press, 265 pp. (SEP cites 2015; some retailers date it late 2015.)
  • DOI / URL: via https://ndpr.nd.edu/news/whats-wrong-with-morality-a-social-psychological-perspective/
  • Access: secondary source named (Carbonell, NDPR, read in full); book not opened. Table of contents and the Hartshorne and May mention via search snippet [N1, N3].
  • Type: book
  • Annotation: Batson's mature statement: moral hypocrisy is "motivation to appear moral while, if possible, avoiding the cost of actually being moral" (p. 94); principles are "vulnerable to rationalization, self-deception, and moral hypocrisy"; remedies are practice, emulation and a broadened outlook. It confirms that Batson's construct stayed a motive construct to the end. Cite via the review and say so.

carbonell2016ndpr

  • Citation: Carbonell, V. (2016, April 10). Review of C. Daniel Batson, What's Wrong with Morality? A Social-Psychological Perspective. Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews, 2016.04.10.
  • DOI / URL: https://ndpr.nd.edu/news/whats-wrong-with-morality-a-social-psychological-perspective/
  • Access: full text read [N1, N3]
  • Type: book review
  • Annotation: Calls the experiments "artificial and game-like", notes that data are "presented only in summary", identifies a principlist bias in Batson's definition of "moral", and reports integrity in only "10–32%" of participants. It is the main expert critique of the programme's evidential base in the notes. For the paper it supports citing Batson for the pattern rather than for precise percentages.

miller2025sep

  • Citation: Miller, C. B. (2015; substantive revision 30 May 2025). Distributive justice and empirical moral psychology. In Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, §3 "Batson's Modified Dictator Game and Moral Hypocrisy".
  • DOI / URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-moral-psych/
  • Access: full text read [N1]
  • Type: encyclopedia entry (secondary)
  • Annotation: The source of nearly every Batson cell number, self-rating and procedural quotation in the notes, each pinned to primary pages. It spells out the three components that separate Batson's hypocrisy from weakness of will, including the self-claim of having acted morally. Because the article bodies were not read, any Batson number the paper uses rests on this entry and must be flagged as such until checked.

carpentermarshall2009

  • Citation: Carpenter, T. P., & Marshall, M. A. (2009). An examination of religious priming and intrinsic religious motivation in the moral hypocrisy paradigm. Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion, 48(2), 386–393.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1111/j.1468-5906.2009.01454.x · https://relbib.de/Record/1646924541
  • Access: metadata only (bibliographic record); results not opened [N1]
  • Type: empirical article
  • Annotation: A direct use of the coin paradigm with religious priming. It shows the paradigm travelled beyond Batson's lab, but its results are not in the notes. Cite only as an example of reuse, if at all.

tamming2017

  • Citation: Tamming, K. (2017). Desiring to appear moral versus being moral: Development of moral hypocrisy and moral integrity (MA thesis). Wilfrid Laurier University.
  • DOI / URL: https://scholars.wlu.ca/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3044&context=etd
  • Access: full text read (first pages and abstract, N1); the 62.2 % children figure via search snippet (N3)
  • Type: thesis
  • Annotation: A developmental extension with 260 children. Its abstract paraphrases Batson's definition in wording that does not match the 1997 text, which is why the notes warn against citing that paraphrase as Batson's. Useful only as a caution about circulating paraphrases.

3. Replications and reinterpretations of the coin paradigm

dong2019selfenhancement

  • Citation: Dong, M., van Prooijen, J.-W., & van Lange, P. A. M. (2019). Self-enhancement in moral hypocrisy: Moral superiority and moral identity are about better appearances. PLoS ONE, 14(7), e0219382.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1371/journal.pone.0219382 · https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0219382 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6611614/
  • Access: full text read [N1, N3]
  • Type: empirical article (3 online studies; Study 3 preregistered)
  • Annotation: Among randomizer users, 68.3 % (n = 101), 88 % (n = 233, verifiable cheating with a randomizer that always assigned the long task) and 69.4 % (n = 242, preregistered) gave themselves the favourable task. It is the best independent evidence that the behavioural core replicates, at a smaller magnitude than 90 %, and it proposes a self-enhancement motive. It is the safest anchor for "repeatedly observed, including in a preregistered sample".

lonnqvist2014

  • Citation: Lönnqvist, J.-E., Irlenbusch, B., & Walkowitz, G. (2014). Moral hypocrisy: Impression management or self-deception? Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 55, 53–62.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1016/j.jesp.2014.06.004 · https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/58707 · https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:zbw:vfsc13:79701 (2013 conference version)
  • Access: abstract (LSE repository record, N1 and N2). N3 records the journal-version N = 256 as search-only and opened the 2013 conference version (N = 320, 4 studies). The LSE abstract is used.
  • Type: empirical article (3 studies, dictator games)
  • Provenance tag: psych-scale (Schwartz values as a descriptive covariate)
  • Annotation: People let a fair coin decide payoffs "only if they could fudge the results", which the authors read as impression management rather than self-deception; hypocrisy tracked Schwartz Conformity and integrity tracked Universalism. It is the main evidence against Batson's mechanism and the one link from hypocrisy to the Schwartz value profile. For the paper it shows that even the human motive is contested, so importing any motive is doubly unsafe.

lin2021dynamic

  • Citation: Lin & Miller (2021). A dynamic perspective on moral choice: Revisiting moral hypocrisy. Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 164, 203–217.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2021.02.005 · https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v164y2021icp203-217.html
  • Access: abstract (IDEAS/RePEc, opened; ScienceDirect returned 403) [N3]
  • Type: empirical article (4 studies)
  • Annotation: Argues that people flip hoping to get the pleasant task without guilt and rationalize only when that outcome is unavailable, so that "moral decisions are more flexible than the hypocrisy interpretation implied". It disputes the motive, not the behaviour. For the paper it is a further reason to describe the gap behaviourally.

sun2025behavsci

  • Citation: Sun, Yang, Fan, Shao, & Tang (2025). [Batson-style task-assignment and self–other judgment studies under cognitive load, ego depletion and mood.] Behavioral Sciences, 15(12), 1683. (Title and first names not recorded in the notes.)
  • DOI / URL: 10.3390/bs15121683 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12729938/
  • Access: full text read (PMC) [N3]
  • Type: empirical article (3 studies, Chinese samples; not preregistered)
  • Annotation: With a preprogrammed "random" draw, 48.0 % (12/25) were hypocrites in the neutral control, 10.7 % under load, 74.1 % under ego depletion and about 80 % under negative mood; a self–other judgment study gave partial conceptual support to the load effect. It is the only non-Western adaptation in the notes and calls for preregistered replication. Cite as conceptual and non-preregistered, with small cells.

4. Judgment asymmetry and power

valdesolo2007groups

  • Citation: Valdesolo, P., & DeSteno, D. (2007). Moral hypocrisy: Social groups and the flexibility of virtue. Psychological Science, 18(8), 689–690.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01961.x · https://news.northeastern.edu/2007/08/08/hypocrisy/
  • Access: metadata (Crossref, OpenAlex); design and results via secondary source named (Northeastern press release, 8 Aug 2007); full text paywalled [N2, N3]
  • Type: empirical report
  • Integrity: failed replication: preregistered direct and conceptual replications did not reproduce it [robertson2024replication]; FLoRA codes it "Failure".
  • Annotation: People judged their own unfair task assignment more mildly than the same act by others and excused in-group members. The intergroup extension failed a well-powered preregistered replication. Cite only alongside that record, never as an established effect.

valdesolo2008duality

  • Citation: Valdesolo, P., & DeSteno, D. (2008). The duality of virtue: Deconstructing the moral hypocrite. Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 44(5), 1334–1338.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1016/j.jesp.2008.03.010 · https://desteno.squarespace.com/s/the-duality-of-virtue-deconstructing-the-moral-hypocrite.pdf
  • Access: full text read (author PDF on DeSteno's site, N3). N2 found the PDF on Valdesolo's CV returned 404 and relied on press releases. N3's full-text numbers are used. Crossref shows no retraction or correction.
  • Type: empirical article (N = 91, about 23 per cell)
  • Integrity: no direct replication found; partial, non-preregistered conceptual support [sun2025behavsci].
  • Annotation: The self-favouring fairness asymmetry (t(49) = 3.39, d = .95) disappeared under a digit-string load (F(1, 87) = 6.20, d = .75), read as effortful justification layered on an even-handed intuitive aversion. The dual-process reading is an analogy at most for models, and the load result rests on small cells. Do not cite it for "hypocrisy is a quick bias that deliberation corrects" as settled.

robertson2024replication

  • Citation: Robertson, Akles, & Van Bavel (2024). Preregistered replication and extension of "Moral hypocrisy: Social groups and the flexibility of virtue". Psychological Science, 35(7), 798–813.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1177/09567976241246552 · https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/09567976241246552/ · https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01961.x/
  • Access: abstract (Crossref, N2); APS journal page opened (N3); FLoRA entry opened (N3)
  • Type: preregistered replication
  • Annotation: A direct replication with minimal groups (N = 610) and a conceptual one with political groups (N = 606) "did not replicate the original findings"; the authors call the evidence for intergroup moral hypocrisy "mixed". It is the clearest failed replication in the hypocrisy family. Cite it whenever Valdesolo and DeSteno 2007 appears.

polman2012anger

  • Citation: Polman & Ruttan (2012). [Title not recorded in the notes.] Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 38(1), 129–139.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1177/0146167211422365 · https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167211422365
  • Access: abstract (Crossref) [N2]
  • Type: empirical article (2 studies)
  • Annotation: In the self-versus-other judgment design, "anger increased moral hypocrisy, guilt eliminated moral hypocrisy, and envy reversed moral hypocrisy", including with real donations. It shows the asymmetry is sensitive to affective state. Peripheral to the paper; no replication recorded.

vantveer2014load

  • Citation: van 't Veer, Stel, & van Beest (2014). [Title not recorded in the notes.] Judgment and Decision Making, 9(3), 199–206.
  • DOI / URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/625EFF3BDE0362B9D3A03B349AF9C7D3/core-reader
  • Access: full text read (Cambridge core reader) [N2]
  • Type: empirical article
  • Annotation: Found "considerable lying when cognitive capacity was not limited" and "no detectable lying when cognitive capacity was limited", and notes that the dual-system framework yields "evidence pointing in opposite directions". It shows the mechanism literature is unsettled. Cite only to qualify dual-process readings.

shalvi2012time

  • Citation: Shalvi, Eldar, & Bereby-Meyer (2012). [Title not recorded in the notes.] Psychological Science, 23(10), 1264–1270.
  • DOI / URL: 10.1177/0956797612443835 · https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/when-do-we-lie-when-were-short-on-time-and-long-on-reasons.html
  • Access: secondary source named (search-surfaced summary of the paper and of two 2020 replications) [N2]
