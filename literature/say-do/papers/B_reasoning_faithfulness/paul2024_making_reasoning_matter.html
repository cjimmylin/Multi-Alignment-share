Paul et al. 2024: Making Reasoning Matter: Measuring and Improving Faithfulness of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

Citation: Paul, D., West, R., Bosselut, A., & Faltings, B. (2024). Making Reasoning Matter: Measuring and Improving Faithfulness of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning. Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2024. arXiv:2402.13950. Venue: Findings of EMNLP 2024 (peer-reviewed; per arXiv comment "Accepted at EMNLP Findings") URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13950 PDF downloaded: yes (paul2024_making_reasoning_matter.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the intermediate reasoning steps, treated as a mediator R.
  • Do: the final answer Y.
  • Faithfulness: the indirect effect of the reasoning on the answer, estimated by causal mediation analysis. The question is whether the answer changes when the reasoning is replaced with counterfactual reasoning.

Method and models

  • Analysis: causal mediation analysis on 12 LLMs, including GPT-4, ChatGPT, GPT-3.5-Instruct, Llama-2-7B-Chat and Mistral-Instruct-7B, across mathematical, commonsense and causal reasoning tasks (e.g., StrategyQA).
  • FRODO: the authors propose a framework that tailors small LMs to generate correct reasoning steps and to reason faithfully over them, trained with causal and counterfactual preference objectives.

Key quantitative findings

  • Large variation in causal reliance (§3, Tables 2–3): instruction-tuned models (GPT-3.5-Instruct, Mistral-Instruct-7B) rely more on their reasoning than RLHF-trained ones (ChatGPT, Llama-2-7B-Chat).
  • GPT-4 (§1, Fig. 1): changes its answer only 30% of the time when conditioned on perturbed counterfactual reasoning chains.
  • Inverse scaling for some tasks (§3): the indirect effect worsens as models get more capable.
  • FRODO: outperforms four baselines and gives rationales more faithful to its predictions than standard SFT.

Authors' explanation

  • LLMs do not reliably use the reasoning steps they generate.
  • RLHF may weaken the causal reliance of answers on reasoning.

Limitations

  • The mediation analysis relies on generated counterfactual reasoning chains, with perturbations produced by GPT-4.
  • FRODO is demonstrated on small LMs.

Relation to our finding

The GPT-4 result is a clean statement of a written analysis failing to govern the decision: given reasoning that implies a different answer, the model keeps its original answer 70% of the time. That is the in-context analogue of "the memo says X, the move is Y". - Difference: in Paul et al. the reasoning is imposed or perturbed by the experimenter. In our episodes the model writes the priority-faithful memo itself, and the action diverges anyway. - Implication: the RLHF-vs-instruction-tuning contrast suggests post-training regime as a moderator of the memo–action gap worth reporting.

Annotation

Paul et al. use causal mediation analysis on 12 LLMs and find that models do not reliably use their own reasoning steps. GPT-4 changes its answer only 30% of the time when given counterfactual reasoning, RLHF models rely on reasoning less than instruction-tuned ones, and some tasks show inverse scaling. The paper offers a causal definition of "reasoning matters" (indirect effect) that suits benchmark designs asking whether a written analysis actually determines the downstream decision.
