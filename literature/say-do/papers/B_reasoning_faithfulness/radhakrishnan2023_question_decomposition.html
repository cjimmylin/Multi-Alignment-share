Radhakrishnan et al. 2023: Question Decomposition Improves the Faithfulness of Model-Generated Reasoning

Citation: Radhakrishnan, A., Nguyen, K., Chen, A., Chen, C., Denison, C., Hernandez, D., Durmus, E., Hubinger, E., Kernion, J., et al. (2023). Question Decomposition Improves the Faithfulness of Model-Generated Reasoning. arXiv:2307.11768. 24 authors, Anthropic (plus J. Brauner, Oxford). Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11768 PDF downloaded: yes (radhakrishnan2023_question_decomposition.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the model's reasoning, in one of three forms: CoT, CoT decomposition (subquestions in one context), or factored decomposition (subquestions answered in separate contexts).
  • Do: the final answer.
  • Faithfulness: measured with the metrics of Lanham et al. (truncation, adding mistakes) and of Turpin et al. (biased-context accuracy drop).

Method and models

  • Model: an RLHF-finetuned model sharing a base with Claude 1.3 (§3), using best-of-N sampling (N=5).
  • Tasks: HotpotQA, OpenBookQA, StrategyQA, TruthfulQA.

Key quantitative findings

  • Accuracy (Table 1, Fig. 1): CoT has the highest accuracy, with factored decomposition somewhat lower. On HotpotQA, for example, CoT reaches 87.3%, CoT decomposition 86.7%, factored decomposition 83.0%, and zero-shot and few-shot 77.0% (§3.1).
  • Corrupted reasoning (§3.2.2): a corrupted reasoning sample changes the final answer for only 9.6% of questions under CoT, against 28.7% under CoT decomposition and 33.6% under factored decomposition. The model relies more on decomposed reasoning.
  • Biased contexts (Suggested Answer, Fig. 5 left): accuracy drops by 21.3% with CoT, 29.1% with CoT decomposition, and 9.2% with factored decomposition. The few-shot baseline drops 16.6%.

Authors' explanation

  • Answering subquestions in isolated contexts keeps spurious information in the original context, such as a biasing suggestion, from leaking into each step.
  • This makes the stated steps more load-bearing.

Limitations

  • There is a performance–faithfulness trade-off (Pareto frontier, Fig. 1).
  • Metrics are proxies; one model family; QA tasks only.

Relation to our finding

This paper shows faithfulness can be engineered by changing the structure of the process, not just by asking for reasoning. - Parallel: our memo is a required written record, much like a CoT that is written but not structurally binding on the action. - Implication for interventions: if the action has to be derived from the memo (for example, tool calls whose arguments must cite memo conclusions, or a separate context that sees only the memo), the right-memo-wrong-move gap might shrink. That is a testable extension. - Difference: their "do" is a QA answer, not an agentic action trading off competing priorities.

Annotation

Radhakrishnan et al. compare CoT with question decomposition and show that factored decomposition makes stated reasoning more causally relevant: corrupted reasoning changes 33.6% of answers against 9.6% for CoT. It also reduces sensitivity to biased contexts (accuracy drop of 9.2% against 21.3% for CoT), at some cost in accuracy. For benchmark design, it shows that the coupling between stated reasoning and behaviour depends on the structure of the pipeline, so how a written record is wired to the action should be reported and varied.
