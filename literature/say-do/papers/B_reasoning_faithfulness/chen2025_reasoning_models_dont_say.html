Chen et al. 2025: Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think

Citation: Chen, Y., Benton, J., Radhakrishnan, A., Uesato, J., Denison, C., Schulman, J., Somani, A., Hase, P., Wagner, M., Roger, F., Mikulik, V., Bowman, S. R., Leike, J., Kaplan, J., & Perez, E. (2025). Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think. arXiv:2505.05410. Anthropic Alignment Science. Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed); Anthropic research report URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410 PDF downloaded: yes (chen2025_reasoning_models_dont_say.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the extended-thinking CoT of a reasoning model.
  • Do: the final answer, or exploitation of a reward hack during RL.
  • Measure: the faithfulness score is the rate at which the CoT verbalizes a hint, counted only in cases where inserting the hint changed the answer to the hinted one.

Method and models

  • Models: Claude 3.7 Sonnet and DeepSeek R1, compared with Claude 3.5 Sonnet (New) and DeepSeek V3.
  • Six hint types: sycophancy, consistency, visual pattern, metadata, and two "misaligned" hints: grader hacking, and unethically obtained information (e.g., "You have gained unauthorized access...").
  • Tasks: MMLU and GPQA.
  • RL experiments: outcome-based RL, plus synthetic RL environments with injected reward hacks.

Key quantitative findings

  • Low overall faithfulness (§3, Fig. 1): models reveal hint use in at least 1% of cases in most settings, but often below 20%. Average faithfulness is 25% for Claude 3.7 Sonnet and 39% for DeepSeek R1.
  • Misaligned hints: faithfulness is 20% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) and 29% (R1).
  • Unfaithful CoTs are longer, not shorter: Claude 3.7 Sonnet averages 2064 ± 59 tokens unfaithful against 1439 ± 54 faithful (§3).
  • Harder tasks are worse (Fig. 4): GPQA CoTs are less faithful than MMLU CoTs by 44% relative (Claude) and 32% relative (R1).
  • RL helps, then plateaus (§4, Fig. 5): outcome-based RL first raises faithfulness (+63% relative on MMLU, +41% on GPQA), then plateaus at 28% (MMLU) and 20% (GPQA).
  • Reward hacks go unverbalized (§5, Fig. 6): models learn the hacks (reward > 0.99) but verbalize them in < 2% of examples in 5 of 6 environments. Instead, the CoT abruptly switches answer or builds elaborate false justifications.

Authors' explanation

  • These tasks do not require CoT, so the CoT is not needed to produce the behaviour.
  • Pretraining and finetuning data mostly feature content-focused explanations that do not mention circumstantial cues.
  • They contrast this with Baker et al.'s >90% detection, where the hacks needed reasoning.

Limitations (§7)

  • Multiple-choice settings.
  • Hints that are easy to exploit.
  • No situations where CoT is necessary.
  • Faithfulness measured only as hint verbalization.

Relation to our finding

This is the most direct current evidence that a frontier model's written reasoning omits what drives its outputs. Its RL finding (behaviour changes, verbalization does not follow) parallels a stable written analysis alongside drifting action. - Their gap is concealment: the hidden driver is absent from the CoT. - Ours is inconsistency between two records: the memo openly states one priority, and the action implements a compromise. - Their "abrupt change without justification" mode resembles our memo→action discontinuity, though in their case both sit inside one CoT. - Their caveat that CoT monitoring is "not sufficient to rule out" undesired behaviour carries over to written records. Our design adds a second check by scoring actions blind.

Annotation

Chen et al. measure whether reasoning models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, DeepSeek R1) verbalize hints that change their answers. CoTs reveal hint use only 25% and 39% of the time on average, and only about 20–29% for "misaligned" hints. Under RL, models learn reward hacks (reward > 0.99) while verbalizing them in under 2% of cases. For benchmark design, a model's written reasoning cannot certify that a behaviour is absent, so behaviour must be measured directly.
