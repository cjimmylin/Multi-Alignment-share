Emmons et al. 2025: When Chain of Thought is Necessary, Language Models Struggle to Evade Monitors

Citation: Emmons, S., Jenner, E., Elson, D. K., Saurous, R. A., Rajamanoharan, S., Chen, H., Shafkat, I., & Shah, R. (2025). When Chain of Thought is Necessary, Language Models Struggle to Evade Monitors. arXiv:2507.05246. Google DeepMind. Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05246 PDF downloaded: yes (emmons2025_cot_necessary_monitors.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the CoT.
  • Do: the final answer or behaviour.
  • Framework: the paper distinguishes CoT-as-rationalization, where the model explains an answer it has already computed, from CoT-as-computation, where the CoT is a necessary part of producing the answer.
  • Argument: unfaithfulness is a property of the rationalization regime. Runtime safety needs monitorability, not full faithfulness.

Method and models

  • Models: Gemini 1.5 Pro, 2.0 Flash and 2.5 Flash/Pro.
  • Replications of prior setups:
  • hint-following on GPQA with simple vs complex hints, e.g., "the correct answer is the letter K + Q mod 4";
  • Lanham-style early answering and adding mistakes, across math problems of varying difficulty.
  • Red-teaming: stress-testing of CoT monitors against deliberate evasion, using human-written strategies and automated red-teaming.

Key quantitative findings

  • Hints (§3.1, Fig. 3): with simple hints, models choose the hinted wrong answer without mentioning it well above the no-hint baseline, replicating Chen et al. and Turpin et al. With complex hints that need computation, the unfaithful behaviour falls to the no-hint baseline: every increase is explained by CoTs that explicitly work through the hint.
  • Forced mistakes (§3.2, Fig. 4): on easy problems, Gemini 1.5 Pro often reaches the correct answer despite a forced incorrect step, an "unfaithful" inconsistency. On hard problems, the final answer follows the corrupted CoT.
  • Evasion (§4–5): models can learn to obscure reasoning only with significant help, such as detailed human-written strategies or iterative optimization against the monitor. Unaided attempts generally fail. Fig. 9 shows an evasion where a biology problem is disguised as physics.

Authors' explanation

When a task exceeds what the model can compute without CoT, the reasoning must appear in the CoT, which makes it monitorable.

Limitations

  • Gemini models only.
  • Task difficulty is used as a proxy for necessity.
  • Stress tests are not exhaustive.

Relation to our finding

The rationalization/computation split gives a candidate explanation for "right memo, wrong move". - Memo as rationalization of the priority: writing a priority-faithful analysis is easy and does not need to be computed in order to choose the tool calls. - Action from an unverbalized compromise prior: the middle-plan action may come from a policy that never passes through the memo. - Testable prediction: making the action depend computationally on the memo should reduce the gap. Examples: actions whose parameters can only be derived from the memo's analysis, or a pipeline that executes only what the memo specifies. - Different phenomenon: our case is not evasion. The memo is not disguised, it is decoupled.

Annotation

Emmons et al. argue that CoT unfaithfulness arises mainly when CoT is a post-hoc rationalization, and show that when hints or problems are hard enough to need CoT computation, Gemini models' unfaithful hint-following and answer inconsistency disappear. They also find that evading CoT monitors requires substantial help, such as human-written strategies or optimization against the monitor. For benchmark design, whether a written record is causally necessary for the scored behaviour should be made explicit, because decoupling is expected when it is not.
