Arcuschin et al. 2025: Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in the Wild Is Not Always Faithful

Citation: Arcuschin, I., Janiak, J., Krzyzanowski, R., Rajamanoharan, S., Nanda, N., & Conmy, A. (2025). Chain-of-Thought Reasoning In The Wild Is Not Always Faithful. arXiv:2503.08679 (v6). Venue: ICML 2026 according to the arXiv comment on v6 ("Published at the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning"). Earlier versions were preprints. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08679 PDF downloaded: yes (arcuschin2025_cot_in_the_wild.pdf, v6)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the CoT, which argues for an answer.
  • Do: the answers to logically paired questions.
  • Implicit Post-Hoc Rationalization (IPHR): across "Is X bigger than Y?" and "Is Y bigger than X?", the model answers Yes/Yes or No/No and builds a plausible argument for each answer. It does this by switching arguments or by selectively misremembering facts, which suggests an implicit Yes/No bias drives the answer.
  • Unfaithful Illogical Shortcuts: on hard math, the model uses an unjustified step while presenting the solution as rigorous.

Method and models

  • IPHR data: 4,834 comparative question pairs built from a world-model dataset, 20 samples per question, judged by an LLM (validated in App. Q).
  • Putnam data: 215 PutnamBench problems with answers that are hard to guess.
  • Models: a broad set of frontier models (GPT-4o-mini, Claude Haiku 3.5, Gemini 1.5/2.5 Pro and Flash, Claude 3.7 Sonnet with thinking, DeepSeek R1, and others), plus three thinking/non-thinking pairs for the shortcuts analysis.

Key quantitative findings

  • IPHR rates (§2, Fig. 2): unfaithful pair rates range from near zero to 13%.
  • Highest: GPT-4o-mini 13.49%, Haiku 3.5 7.42%, Gemini 1.5 Pro 6.54%.
  • Lowest: Claude 3.7 Sonnet with 1,024-token thinking at 0.04% (2 pairs); Gemini 2.5 Pro at 0.14%. The abstract also reports DeepSeek R1 at 0.37%.
  • Robustness: 76% of unfaithful pairs are retained when resampling with 100 responses per question.
  • Argument switching: across 8 models with ≥30 unfaithful pairs, a median 18% show argument switching without any fact inconsistency, and argument switching appears in a median 45% overall. The remaining 52% show only biased fact inconsistency.
  • Illogical shortcuts (§3): found in thinking and non-thinking models from Anthropic, DeepSeek and Qwen. The thinking vs non-thinking gap is about 17–19 percentage points under two judges (robustness check, App. Q.2).

Authors' explanation

  • Implicit biases toward Yes or No, or toward certain answers, settle the answer first.
  • The CoT then selects facts and arguments to support it.
  • On math, RL that rewards final answers may reward rigorous-looking but invalid shortcuts.

Limitations (§5.1)

  • IPHR shows unfaithfulness only through cross-question inconsistency; the true cause is inferred.
  • Results depend on an LLM judge.
  • The Putnam analysis carries some contamination risk.

Relation to our finding

This is the closest "in the wild" analogue: no adversarial cue is needed, and coherent arguments are built for a conclusion settled by other means. - Possible shared mechanism: our middle-plan actions may come from a learned prior toward compromise or hedging, the action-level counterpart of an implicit Yes bias. - Where the rationalization sits differs: - in IPHR the rationalization supports the conclusion actually given; - in our episodes the memo supports the stated priority, and the action follows the prior. - Similar detection method: like IPHR, our gap is found by comparing two artefacts, not by reading one CoT. In our case the artefacts are the memo and the tool calls, scored blind.

Annotation

Arcuschin et al. show unfaithful CoT on natural, non-adversarial prompts. Models answer logically opposite comparative questions identically and build plausible arguments for both, at rates up to 13% (GPT-4o-mini) falling to 0.04% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet thinking). They also find illogical shortcuts in proofs presented as rigorous. For benchmark design, the paper shows that paired-item consistency checks can expose post-hoc rationalization without any planted bias, and that frontier models reduce but do not remove it.
