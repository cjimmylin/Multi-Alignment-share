Tutek et al. 2025: Measuring Chain of Thought Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps

Citation: Tutek, M., Hashemi Chaleshtori, F., Marasović, A., & Belinkov, Y. (2025). Measuring Chain of Thought Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps. Proceedings of EMNLP 2025. arXiv:2502.14829. Venue: EMNLP 2025 (peer-reviewed; Outstanding Paper award per arXiv comment) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14829 PDF downloaded: yes (tutek2025_unlearning_reasoning_steps.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: individual CoT steps.
  • Do: the model's prediction.
  • Parametric faithfulness: a step is faithful if erasing the knowledge it expresses from the model's parameters (via unlearning) changes the prediction. This contrasts with "contextual" methods that only edit the CoT text, such as Lanham's adding mistakes.

Method and models

  • Framework: PFF (Parametric Faithfulness Framework), instantiated as FUR (Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning steps) using the NPO unlearning method.
  • Metrics: FF-HARD (is the whole CoT faithful?) and FF-SOFT (which steps are most salient?).
  • Models: four LMs (LLaMA-3-8B, LLaMA-3B, Mistral-2, Phi-3).
  • Datasets: five multi-hop multiple-choice datasets (ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA, Sports, StrategyQA, TruthfulQA).
  • Human study: a user study of plausibility.

Key quantitative findings

  • FUR vs Add-mistake (Table 2, §6): unlearning single steps changes predictions in a "reasonably high" share of instances, and generally finds more faithful CoTs than the contextual Add-mistake baseline. The authors conclude that contextual tests may underestimate faithfulness.
  • Effect on reasoning: unlearning changes the answer that post-unlearning verbalized reasoning supports.
  • Faithfulness vs plausibility (§6.4): the Pearson correlation between FF-SOFT step importance and human ratings of supportiveness is only 0.15. Steps that matter causally are not the ones humans find plausible.

Authors' explanation

  • Erasing text from the context does not remove the knowledge from the parameters, so contextual tests are imprecise.
  • Alignment for plausibility and alignment for faithfulness are different targets.

Limitations

  • Small open models only.
  • Unlearning efficacy and specificity vary by dataset, e.g., Sports has knowledge overlap between instances.
  • Multiple-choice QA only.

Relation to our finding

Tutek adds a nuance: some textual tests may overstate unfaithfulness, and what makes reasoning look good to humans is nearly uncorrelated with what drives the answer (r = 0.15). - Second point for us: our judged memos are scored for reasons quality, which is closer to plausibility. A high reasons score therefore does not imply that the reasons drive the action. This is consistent with our observed decoupling. - Difference: Tutek studies knowledge steps in QA, not value priorities in agentic choice.

Annotation

Tutek et al. measure CoT faithfulness by unlearning the knowledge in individual reasoning steps from model parameters (FUR) and checking whether predictions change. They find that parametric interventions often flip predictions and that contextual perturbation tests may underestimate faithfulness. Steps that are causally important correlate only weakly (r = 0.15) with human plausibility ratings, a caution for any benchmark that scores the quality of written reasons as a proxy for what drives behaviour.
