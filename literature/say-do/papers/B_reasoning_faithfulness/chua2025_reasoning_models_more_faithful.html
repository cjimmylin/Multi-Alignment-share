Chua & Evans 2025: Are DeepSeek R1 and Other Reasoning Models More Faithful?

Citation: Chua, J., & Evans, O. (2025). Are DeepSeek R1 And Other Reasoning Models More Faithful? arXiv:2501.08156 (v5, July 2025). Truthful AI / UC Berkeley. Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed; no venue listed on arXiv) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08156 PDF downloaded: yes (chua2025_reasoning_models_more_faithful.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the CoT.
  • Do: the MMLU answer.
  • Faithfulness: whether the model articulates a cue (e.g., "A Stanford Professor thinks the answer is D") in cases where that cue switched its answer. This follows Turpin et al.

Method and models

  • Reasoning models: QwQ-32B-Preview, Gemini-2.0-flash-thinking-exp, DeepSeek R1.
  • Comparisons: their non-reasoning counterparts (Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct, Gemini-2.0-flash-exp, DeepSeek V3) plus Claude-3.5-Sonnet and GPT-4o.
  • Seven cue types, including professor, black-square few-shot, white-square, argument, post-hoc, wrong few-shot, and "Are you sure?".
  • Additional experiment: reward-model preferences between faithful and unfaithful CoTs.

Key quantitative findings

  • Professor cue (Fig. 1, §3): R1 articulates the cue 59% of the time against 7% for DeepSeek V3. Gemini thinking reaches 68% and QwQ 47%. The best non-reasoning model reaches 13%.
  • Black-square cue (Fig. 4): Claude-3.5-Sonnet articulates 3%, and reasoning models at least 17%.
  • Across cue types: non-reasoning models frequently score 0%.
  • Reward models (§4): the reward models used to finetune non-reasoning models prefer unfaithful responses. The authors offer this as a possible explanation for why non-reasoning models are less faithful.

Authors' explanation

  • RL on reasoning tasks, without reward-model pressure on the CoT, may preserve verbalization of influences.
  • RLHF reward models may penalize admitting to being swayed.

Limitations (stated in abstract)

  • Artificial tasks.
  • Only one aspect of faithfulness (articulating cues) is measured.

Relation to our finding

This paper complicates any blanket "reasoning makes it worse" story: reasoning models were more candid about cues in this test. Note that Chen et al. 2025 later found that even reasoning models verbalize hints only 25–39% of the time. - Implication for us: if we compare model classes, reasoning models need not show a smaller memo–action gap, because articulating an influence and acting consistently with a stated conclusion are different properties. - Differences: Chua's "say" is the CoT about the cue and "do" is the MCQ answer. Our memo is a required deliverable whose content is graded, which may invite approval-seeking. That is exactly the pressure Chua links to reward models favouring unfaithfulness.

Annotation

Chua & Evans test whether reasoning models articulate prompt cues that change their answers. DeepSeek R1 does so 59% of the time against 7% for DeepSeek V3, and reasoning models from three developers beat all non-reasoning models tested. They also find that reward models prefer unfaithful CoTs, offering a mechanism for RLHF-induced unfaithfulness. For benchmark design, faithfulness differs by model class and training regime, so results on stated-versus-actual reasoning should be broken out by reasoning and non-reasoning models.
