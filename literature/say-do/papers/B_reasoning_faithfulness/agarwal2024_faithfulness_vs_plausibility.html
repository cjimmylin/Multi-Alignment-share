Agarwal et al. 2024: Faithfulness vs. Plausibility: On the (Un)Reliability of Explanations from Large Language Models

Citation: Agarwal, C., Tanneru, S. H., & Lakkaraju, H. (2024). Faithfulness vs. Plausibility: On the (Un)Reliability of Explanations from Large Language Models. arXiv:2402.04614. Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed; position/perspective paper) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04614 PDF downloaded: yes (agarwal2024_faithfulness_vs_plausibility.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Conceptual. Say: LLM self-explanations (CoT, token importance, counterfactual explanations). Do: the model's actual reasoning or decision process.
  • Plausibility: how convincing an explanation is to humans.
  • Faithfulness: whether it reflects the model's actual decision process.

Method

Position paper with literature synthesis (§§2–6); no new large-scale experiments.

Key claims

  • LLMs are adept at plausible explanations that need not align with their reasoning (abstract, §5).
  • §5.1: the push toward user-friendly, plausible explanations may come at the cost of faithfulness.
  • §5.2–5.3: the risks of plausible-but-unfaithful explanations (misplaced trust) and of faithful-but-implausible ones are discussed.
  • §5.4 and §6, use-case dependence:
  • high-stakes domains (healthcare, finance, crime forecasting) require faithfulness;
  • interactive or creative uses (education, storytelling) may prioritize plausibility.
  • The paper calls for methods to characterize and improve faithfulness.

Authors' explanation

  • Training on human-written text and human preference feedback optimizes how explanations look.
  • Nothing optimizes their correspondence to internal computation.

Limitations

Perspective paper; claims are argued, not newly measured.

Relation to our finding

This paper frames the risk that motivates our scoring split. Our judged written record is evaluated in a way that resembles plausibility (is the analysis sound and faithful to the priority?), while the action reveals the operative policy. - What our data add: concrete agentic evidence that a high-plausibility record and a non-compliant action can co-occur. - Use-case argument: this supports treating action-consistency as the primary metric in high-stakes agentic deployments.

Annotation

Agarwal, Tanneru & Lakkaraju argue that LLM self-explanations are optimized for plausibility to humans, not faithfulness to the model's reasoning, and that pursuing plausibility may erode faithfulness. They argue for prioritizing faithfulness in high-stakes uses. For benchmark design, the paper justifies scoring the persuasiveness of written reasons separately from the behaviour they purport to explain.
