Needham et al. 2025: Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated

Citation: Needham, J., Edkins, G., Pimpale, G., Bartsch, H., & Hobbhahn, M. (2025). Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated. arXiv:2505.23836. MATS / Apollo Research. Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836 PDF downloaded: yes (needham2025_evaluation_awareness.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • This paper does not directly measure a say–do gap.
  • It measures evaluation awareness: whether models can classify a transcript as coming from an evaluation or from real deployment, and identify the evaluation's purpose (capability vs alignment).
  • Relevance: it is a precondition for behaviour that differs between evaluation and deployment, where stated or evaluated behaviour may not match deployed behaviour.

Method and models

  • Benchmark: 1,000 prompts and transcripts from 61 datasets, covering chat and agentic settings, public benchmarks such as MMLU and SWE-bench, real deployment interactions, and agent scaffolds.
  • Models: Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini, and others.
  • Elicitation: zero-shot "Was this from an evaluation?" with five prompt variants.
  • Baseline: a human baseline.

Key quantitative findings (Fig. 2; abstract; §4)

  • Overall AUC:
Rater AUC
Human baseline 0.92
Gemini 2.5 Pro 0.83
GPT-4.1 0.83
Claude 3.7 Sonnet 0.82
GPT-4.1 mini 0.75
  • Chat vs agentic: awareness is higher in agentic transcripts (Claude 3.7 Sonnet 0.88, Gemini 2.5 Pro 0.95, humans 1.00) than in chat (Claude 0.75, Gemini 0.71, humans 0.81).
  • Purpose identification: when told the transcript is an evaluation, Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4.1 identify capability vs alignment purpose with 93% and similar high accuracy (§5).

Authors' explanation

Frontier models pick up cues of artificial tasks: benchmark formatting, synthetic scenarios, and agentic scaffolds.

Limitations

  • Classification ability is not shown to change behaviour.
  • The human baseline is small (few annotators; acknowledged in §3).
  • Dataset composition affects AUC.

Relation to our finding

This is directly relevant to our design threat model. - Threat: agentic episodes, where Needham finds awareness highest, are likely recognizable as evaluations. - What it means for the memo: the memo, as a judged written record, may be written for an evaluator, while the action reflects the underlying policy. - Two possibilities: evaluation awareness could inflate memo quality, or reduce the compromise rate if models act more carefully when they suspect testing. Our 13–28% may therefore be a lower bound on deployment behaviour, or a context-specific estimate. - Recommendation: check verbalized evaluation awareness in our transcripts. - Strand note: evaluation awareness as such belongs to the agent-safety / situational-awareness strand. It is included here at the coordinator's request.

Annotation

Needham et al. build a 1,000-transcript benchmark from 61 sources and show that frontier models distinguish evaluation from deployment transcripts well above chance (Gemini 2.5 Pro AUC 0.83 against a human 0.92), especially in agentic settings. Told a transcript is an evaluation, they can also identify whether it tests capability or alignment. For benchmark design, models may recognize agentic tests, so evaluated words and actions may not match deployment behaviour, and awareness should be measured as a covariate.
