Binder et al. 2024: Looking Inward: Language Models Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection

Citation: Binder, F. J., Chua, J., Korbak, T., Sleight, H., Hughes, J., Long, R., Perez, E., Turpin, M., & Evans, O. (2024). Looking Inward: Language Models Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection. arXiv:2410.13787. Venue: arXiv preprint (v1, Oct 2024). A later conference version may exist but was not verified here; treat as preprint. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787 PDF downloaded: yes (binder2024_looking_inward_introspection.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the model's prediction about a property of its own behaviour in a hypothetical scenario. Example: "Given input P, would your output favor the short- or long-term option?" or "What would the second character of your answer be?"
  • Do: the model's actual behaviour on P.
  • Introspection test: if model M1 has privileged access, M1 should predict M1 better than another model M2 trained on M1's ground-truth behaviour.

Method and models

  • Models: GPT-4, GPT-4o, GPT-3.5 and Llama-3.1-70B, finetuned for self-prediction.
  • Cross-prediction: M2 models are trained on M1's behaviour.
  • Behaviour shift: M1's ground-truth behaviour is modified by further finetuning, to see whether self-predictions track the change.
  • Generalization: tested on more complex and out-of-distribution tasks.

Key quantitative findings

  • Untrained self-prediction is poor (§3.1, Fig. 4): before training, models are close to a most-common-behaviour baseline. Training raises GPT-4o's held-out accuracy from 32.6% to 49.4%.
  • Self beats other (§3.2, Fig. 5): M1 predicts itself better than M2. With Llama-3-70B as M1 and GPT-4o as M2, M1's advantage is +17%.
  • Tracking change: M1 continues to predict itself accurately after its behaviour is intentionally changed.
  • Limits: introspection is elicited on simple tasks but fails on more complex tasks or those requiring out-of-distribution generalization (abstract, §5). For example, it does not generalize to situational-awareness-type tasks.

Authors' explanation

Models may have privileged access to their own behavioural dispositions, i.e., some form of self-simulation, which is not derivable from training data.

Limitations

  • Simple, short-horizon behaviour properties.
  • Introspective ability is small and requires finetuning.
  • It fails to generalize to complex tasks.

Relation to our finding

This is the nearest evidence on whether models can know what they will do. - What it implies for us: if self-prediction is weak without training and fails on complex tasks, a model writing a priority-faithful memo may lack reliable access to whether its subsequent multi-step agentic policy will honour it. - Two readings of the gap: - a memo-writing process with no access to the acting policy; - an acting policy that does not consult the memo. - Our task differs: the memo is normative ("what should be done"), not a self-prediction. Agentic episodes are exactly the complex regime where Binder et al. found introspection fails.

Annotation

Binder et al. finetune LLMs to predict properties of their own hypothetical behaviour. Models predict themselves better than other models trained on their behaviour (e.g., +17% for Llama-3-70B over GPT-4o) and track deliberate changes to their behaviour, which is evidence of limited introspection. The ability is weak before training (GPT-4o 32.6% → 49.4% after) and fails on complex or out-of-distribution tasks, a caution against treating models' statements about their own future conduct as reliable in complex agentic settings.
