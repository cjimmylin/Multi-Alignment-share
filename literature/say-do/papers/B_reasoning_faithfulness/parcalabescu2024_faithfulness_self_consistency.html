Parcalabescu & Frank 2024: On Measuring Faithfulness or Self-consistency of Natural Language Explanations

Citation: Parcalabescu, L., & Frank, A. (2024). On Measuring Faithfulness or Self-consistency of Natural Language Explanations. Proceedings of the 62nd Annual Meeting of the ACL (Volume 1: Long Papers), ACL 2024. arXiv:2311.07466. Venue: ACL 2024 Main (peer-reviewed; per arXiv comment) URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07466 PDF downloaded: yes (parcalabescu2024_faithfulness_self_consistency.pdf)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the natural-language explanation (post-hoc or CoT).
  • Do: the predicted answer.
  • Central claim: existing "faithfulness tests" (counterfactual edits, biasing features, corrupting CoT, etc.) actually measure self-consistency at the output level, not faithfulness to inner workings.

Method and models

  • Comparative Consistency Bank (CCB): compares existing tests on a common suite of 11 open LLMs and 5 tasks (including e-SNLI and ComVE).
  • CC-SHAP: a new fine-grained measure comparing how inputs contribute (via SHAP) to answer generation versus explanation generation.

Key quantitative findings (§§5–6, CCB analysis)

  • Chat LLMs show higher self-consistency than their base variants.
  • CC-SHAP agrees most with the Counterfactual Edits test.
  • The authors could neither detect nor exclude a relationship between model size and self-consistency.
  • Different tests often disagree with each other on the same models.

Authors' explanation

Tests compare outputs before and after input edits and lack access to internals. They therefore certify consistency, and "the quest for true faithfulness metrics remains open".

Limitations

  • Open models only (no frontier APIs).
  • CC-SHAP is itself an input-attribution proxy.

Relation to our finding

This is a terminological guardrail for our paper. - What our design measures: it compares a model's written record with its action, i.e., self-consistency between two behaviours. It does not measure faithfulness to internal reasoning. - How to phrase our claim: we should describe "right memo, wrong move" as an inconsistency between stated priority/analysis and enacted action, not as proof of unfaithful internal reasoning. - Methodological lesson: the paper's finding that different tests disagree supports measuring multiple channels (action and reasons) separately.

Annotation

Parcalabescu & Frank argue that most LLM "faithfulness" tests measure output-level self-consistency, not faithfulness to internal reasoning. They benchmark those tests side by side on 11 open LLMs and introduce CC-SHAP, which compares input contributions to answers versus explanations. For benchmark design, the paper sets out what behavioural say–do comparisons can and cannot claim.
