Madsen et al. 2024: Are Self-Explanations from Large Language Models Faithful?

Citation: Madsen, A., Chandar, S., & Reddy, S. (2024). Are self-explanations from Large Language Models faithful? In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2024, pp. 295–337. doi:10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.19. arXiv:2401.07927. Venue: Findings of ACL 2024 (peer-reviewed) URL: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.19/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07927 PDF downloaded: yes (madsen2024_self_explanations_faithful.pdf, arXiv v4)

Say–do construct

  • Say: the model's self-explanation of a classification. Three types are tested:
  • a counterfactual edit ("edit the input so you'd answer the opposite");
  • feature attribution ("which words were important?");
  • redaction ("remove the words needed to decide").
  • Do: the model's actual classification when given the edited or redacted input.
  • Faithfulness: measured by self-consistency checks. For example, if the model says certain words are important, removing them should impair its prediction.

Method and models

  • Models: Llama2 (70B, 7B), Falcon (40B, 7B), Mistral (7B), instruction-tuned.
  • Tasks: IMDB sentiment, bAbI-1 and MCTest multiple-choice QA, RTE entailment.

Key quantitative findings

  • Explanation-, model- and task-dependent (abstract, §6): no explanation type is reliably faithful.
  • IMDB example: counterfactuals are most faithful for Llama2, feature attribution for Mistral, and redaction for Falcon 40B.
  • Llama2-70B: counterfactuals work only on IMDB, and feature attribution only on RTE and bAbI.
  • Conclusion: self-explanations "should not be trusted in general".

Authors' explanation

  • Models generate explanations that look like human explanations, without reliable access to their own decision basis.
  • Faithfulness is therefore incidental and varies with model and task.

Limitations

  • Classification tasks only.
  • Self-consistency is necessary but not sufficient for faithfulness.
  • Older open models.

Relation to our finding

Madsen et al. directly test whether a model's claims about what it would do under modified inputs match what it does. It is the self-explanation version of a say–do test, and the answer is often no. - Contrast with our design: our models are not asked to predict their actions. They write an analysis of what should be done, then act. Our gap is between prescription and execution, not between self-prediction and behaviour. - Shared methodology: both use behaviour checked against the model's own text as ground truth instead of human judgement of plausibility.

Annotation

Madsen et al. evaluate counterfactual, feature-attribution and redaction self-explanations from Llama2, Falcon and Mistral using self-consistency checks. Faithfulness depends heavily on explanation type, model and task, so LLM self-explanations should not be trusted by default. For benchmark design, the paper shows the value of scoring a model's statements against its own subsequent behaviour, not against human plausibility judgements.
