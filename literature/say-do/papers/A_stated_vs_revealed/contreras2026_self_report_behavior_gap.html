Contreras (2026) — An LLM-Native Psychometric Instrument Reveals a Self-Report–Behavior Gap Across 25 Models

  • Citation: Juan Manuel Contreras. "An LLM-Native Psychometric Instrument Reveals a Self-Report–Behavior Gap Across 25 Models." arXiv:2606.09843 (v4, Oct 2026).
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed; single independent author).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09843
  • PDF downloaded: yes — contreras2026_self_report_behavior_gap.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Say: answers to 300 self-report items about LLM-specific behaviours: sycophantic agreement, over-refusal, unsolicited elaboration, epistemic hedging. A factor structure is induced bottom-up, yielding five factors: Responsiveness, Deference, Boldness, Guardedness, Verbosity.
  • Do: 2,500 open-ended behavioural samples rated on the same constructs by 151 human raters, a three-model LLM-judge ensemble, and rater-free text measures.

Method and models

  • Models: 25 LLMs from 17 developers. Each item is answered 30 times.
  • Psychometrics: factors replicate across split halves, model-level aggregation and leave-one-family-out refits (Tucker φ ≥ .957; α ≥ .930).

Key quantitative findings

  • Humans and LLM judges agree about how models behave (r̄ = .51).
  • Self-report barely tracks behaviour.
  • Against human ratings: r̄ = .09 (95% CI −.07 to .18).
  • Rater-free text measures tell the same story.
  • After correcting for criterion unreliability, four of five factors stay near zero.
  • Verbosity is the partial exception (r = .40, 71% of its reliability ceiling).
  • On Responsiveness, self-report tracks LLM judges more than humans (r = .53 vs .18; Steiger p = .04).

Authors' explanation

  • The gap cannot be blamed on borrowed human trait vocabulary, because the constructs come from LLM behaviour itself.
  • LLM self-description is consistent but uninformative about behaviour.

Limitations

  • Single author; preprint.
  • Behaviours are conversational styles, not values or decisions.
  • LLM-judge agreement with LLM self-report may be shared bias.

Relation to our finding

  • Strengthens the general claim that model self-descriptions do not predict behaviour, even on LLM-native constructs.
  • The judge-vs-human divergence is a methodological caution for our judged-reasons channel. LLM judges may share the self-report's framing, so human audits of judges matter.
  • Differences: style traits rather than value priorities, and no agentic actions.

Annotation

Contreras builds a bottom-up, LLM-native self-report instrument (300 items; five reliable factors) and administers it to 25 models. Self-reports barely correlate with human ratings of 2,500 open-ended behaviour samples (r̄ = .09), even though humans and LLM judges agree with each other (r̄ = .51). Only Verbosity partially tracks behaviour. This rules out "human constructs don't fit LLMs" as the explanation for self-report/behaviour gaps.
