Han et al. (2025) — The Personality Illusion: Revealing Dissociation Between Self-Reports & Behavior in LLMs

  • Citation: Pengrui Han, Rafal Kocielnik, Peiyang Song, Ramit Debnath, Dean Mobbs, Anima Anandkumar, R. Michael Alvarez. "The Personality Illusion: Revealing Dissociation Between Self-Reports & Behavior in LLMs." arXiv:2509.03730 (v2, Sep 2025).
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed at version read).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03730 · code: https://github.com/psychology-of-AI/Personality-Illusion
  • PDF downloaded: yes — han2025_personality_illusion.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Say: self-reported traits on Big Five and self-regulation questionnaires.
  • Do: behavioural tasks adapted from psychology with known trait links: risk-taking, social bias (IAT-style), honesty, sycophancy.
  • Persona injection: prompting toward a trait, which tests whether steering changes self-reports, behaviour, or both.

Method and models

  • Models: 18 — six small base models, their six instruct counterparts, and six large instruct models (Table 1).
  • Analysis: mixed-effects regressions; trait–task associations compared in sign with human expectations.

Key quantitative findings

  • Alignment stabilises self-reports. Instruction tuning / RLHF reduces trait variability by 40.0% (Big Five) and 45.1% (self-regulation), and trait intercorrelations become human-like (§2.3).
  • Self-reports poorly predict behaviour.
  • Only about 24% of trait–task associations are significant.
  • Of those, only 52% match the human-expected direction (chance = 50%).
  • Per-trait alignment is 45–62%, with all 95% CIs overlapping 50%.
  • Persona injection changes words, not behaviour.
  • Self-reports move as intended: agreeableness β = 3.95, p < .001.
  • Expected behaviour barely moves: sycophancy β = 0.03, p = 0.67.

Authors' explanation

  • "Current alignment methods such as RLHF refine linguistic plausibility without grounding it in behavioral regularity."
  • There is a dissociation between "linguistic self-expression and behavioral consistency".

Limitations

  • Big Five traits predict specific behaviours weakly even in humans (r ≈ .20). The follow-up, Kocielnik et al. (2026), addresses this.
  • Self-reports and tasks were run in separate sessions.
  • Personality traits, not values.

Relation to our finding

  • The clearest evidence for "persona steering changes words but not choices": prompting reliably shifts what the model says about itself but not what it does.
  • Analogy to our result: an instruction-assigned priority shapes the analysis (words) more reliably than the action under pressure.
  • Differences:
  • Personality, not values or priorities.
  • Self-report questionnaires, not task-embedded analyses.
  • No competing-pressure manipulation.

Annotation

Han et al. track Big Five and self-regulation self-reports across 18 base and aligned LLMs and test them against behavioural paradigms (risk-taking, bias, honesty, sycophancy). Alignment makes self-reports stable and human-like, but they predict behaviour at chance level (about 24% significant associations, 52% sign-correct). Persona prompts shift self-reports (β = 3.95) but not behaviour (sycophancy β = 0.03). A key citation for "steering changes words, not deeds".
