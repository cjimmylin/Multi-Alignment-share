Xu et al. (2025) — Large Language Models Often Say One Thing and Do Another

  • Citation: Ruoxi Xu, Hongyu Lin, Xianpei Han, Jia Zheng, Weixiang Zhou, Le Sun, Yingfei Sun. "Large Language Models Often Say One Thing and Do Another." ICLR 2025. arXiv:2503.07003.
  • Venue: ICLR 2025 (peer-reviewed).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07003 · code: https://github.com/icip-cas/Word-Deed-Consistency-Test
  • PDF downloaded: yes — xu2025_say_one_thing_do_another.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Benchmark: the Words and Deeds Consistency Test (WDCT), 1,225 paired items (§2.4, Table 2).
  • Word: a direct question probing an opinion, value or theory.
  • Deed: a strictly corresponding scenario question whose option reveals the same stance in action.
  • Domains: opinion vs action; non-ethical value vs action; ethical value vs action; theory vs application.
  • Consistency Score (CS): P(same answer to word and deed). Random baseline = 0.50. A probability-based version (PCS) is also reported.

Method and models

  • Models: GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and base vs chat pairs of Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B and ChatGLM3-6B (Table 5).
  • Prompting: direct and CoT. Option orders shuffled; three runs.
  • Alignment experiments: SFT/DPO separately on words or on deeds (in opposite directions), to see whether alignment transfers.

Key quantitative findings

  • Most LLMs show average inconsistency above 30% against a 50% random baseline (§4.1, Table 3).
  • Base vs aligned models: pretrained LLMs have CS ≈ 0.6 with PCS ≈ 0.9, meaning no strong beliefs. After alignment, PCS drops by about 0.2, meaning the word and deed distributions diverge further (§4.1).
  • CoT barely helps (Table 4, direct → CoT):
Model Direct CoT
GPT-4 0.76 0.79
GPT-3.5 0.66 0.70
Mistral-7B-Instruct 0.66 0.69
ChatGLM3-6B 0.54 0.48
Llama-2-7B-Chat 0.49 0.48
  • Aligning only words, or only deeds, has "poor and unpredictable" effects on the other (Fig. 3).
  • Data augmentation (Dual-Aug) helps but does not solve the problem; e.g., GPT-3.5-scale models rise from 0.71 to 0.86 (Table 4 right).
  • Deeds are more stable than words across paraphrases (Fig. 7).

Authors' explanation

  • Base models lack strong beliefs.
  • In aligned models, words and deeds are aligned independently during post-training.
  • They hypothesise that "the underlying knowledge guiding LLMs' word or deed choices is not contained within a unified space".

Limitations

  • "Deeds" are multiple-choice answers to scenario questions, not actions.
  • Mostly small open models plus two GPT models (2024 vintage).
  • Items are LLM-generated with human review, and about 15% were rewritten.

Relation to our finding

  • Shared: this is the cleanest benchmark-scale evidence that word-level and deed-level answers come apart. The finding that CoT does not repair consistency resonates with ours: models write analyses faithful to the priority yet still act otherwise.
  • The "separately aligned spaces" hypothesis offers one mechanism for why a faithful analysis does not bind the action.
  • Differences:
  • The "word" is the model's own opinion, not an assigned priority.
  • Deeds are hypothetical choices.
  • No competing-pressure manipulation and no reasons scoring.
  • Inconsistency here is roughly symmetric noise. Ours is directional: compromise toward the competing pressure, usually a middle plan.

Annotation

Xu et al.'s WDCT pairs 1,225 "word" questions (opinions, values, theories) with strictly matched "deed" scenario questions. Most LLMs agree with themselves only 50–79% of the time (random = 50%). Alignment widens the word/deed probability gap, and CoT does not help. Fine-tuning only words or only deeds transfers unpredictably, which suggests the two are governed by separately shaped knowledge. A standard ICLR citation for the claim that what LLMs say is not what they do in scenario questions.
