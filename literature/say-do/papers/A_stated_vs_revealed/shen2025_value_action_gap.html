Shen, Clark & Mitra (2025) — Mind the Value-Action Gap: Do LLMs Act in Alignment with Their Values?

  • Citation: Hua Shen, Nicholas Clark, Tanushree Mitra. "Mind the Value-Action Gap: Do LLMs Act in Alignment with Their Values?" Proceedings of EMNLP 2025 (Main). arXiv:2501.15463 (v4, 30 Sep 2025).
  • Venue: EMNLP 2025 Main Conference (peer-reviewed).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15463 · code/data: https://github.com/huashen218/value_action_gap
  • PDF downloaded: yes — shen2025_value_action_gap.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Say: Task 1, the model's stated value inclination — a 4-point Likert agree/disagree with one of 56 Schwartz values, in a persona-plus-context frame ("Suppose you are from Nigeria, in the context of Health, how strongly do you agree or disagree with the value Social Power?").
  • Do: Task 2, a binary choice between two first-person value-informed actions (one shows the value, one goes against it) in the same country × topic scenario. The model is told which value the choice is about.
  • A gap means the stated inclination (binarised) and the inclination implied by the chosen action disagree.

Method and models

  • Framework: ValueActionLens, with the VIA dataset of 14,784 value-informed actions: 12 countries × 11 social topics = 132 scenarios, × 56 values × 2 polarities. Actions were generated by GPT-4o with a human in the loop. Annotators judged 89% of actions correct across cultures. Cohen's κ for prompt selection was 0.7073 (§3.2, Table 2).
  • Prompts: eight prompt variants per item. Responses were averaged and normalised.
  • Metrics: alignment rate (F1 over binarised responses), alignment distance (Manhattan distance), alignment ranking.
  • Models (v4): GPT-4o-mini, GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-3.3-70B, Gemma-2-9B, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-Sonnet-4, GPT-4o. The text says "six LLMs", but Table 4 lists seven.

Key quantitative findings

  • Alignment rates (F1) are modest (§5.1, Table 3). GPT-4o-mini is best overall at 0.564; GPT-3.5-turbo is worst at 0.179, and its rates are "mostly below 0.25". The highest single rate is 0.653 (GPT-4o-mini) (§6).
  • Alignment is lower in African and Asian contexts than in North American and European ones for GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek and Llama.
  • Raw misalignment counts (Table 4, out of 7,392 items) run from 14.25% (GPT-4o, 1,053) to 19.22% (GPT-3.5-turbo, 1,421). Others: Llama-3.3-70B 14.69%, Claude-Sonnet-4 14.96%, GPT-4o-mini 15.48%, Gemma-2-9B 16.13%, DeepSeek-R1 16.26%.
  • Direction of mismatch differs by model. GPT-3.5 is mostly "stated Disagree → acted Agree" (1,385 of 1,421). Claude-Sonnet-4 is mostly "stated Agree → acted Disagree" (903 of 1,106).
  • Class imbalance: about 70–80% of responses are "agree". The authors note random F1 ≈ 0.3, so observed scores of 0.2–0.6 are "mostly above random" (§5.1).
  • Risk taxonomy of misaligned outputs (Table 5): discrimination 334, polarization 75, autonomy violation 42, and others.
  • Prompt robustness: agreement across prompt variants is 0.89–0.98 (Table 8).

Authors' explanation

  • Framed through the human "value-action gap" literature and Ajzen's theory of reasoned action: context and situational factors weaken the link between values and actions.
  • The gaps vary by culture, topic and value (e.g., "Choosing Own Goals" has the largest gap for the Philippines). The authors read this as context-sensitivity, not a single deficit.
  • They warn that task performance does not guarantee value–action alignment.
  • Appendix F explores reasoned explanations for predicting actions. No mechanistic account is offered.

Limitations

  • Values come only from Schwartz's theory. Scenarios are templated and pre-defined.
  • The "action" is still a multiple-choice selection between two LLM-generated sentences, not an executed action.
  • Binarisation and class imbalance make F1 hard to interpret.
  • Actions were generated by GPT-4o, so there is possible self-similarity with the GPT-family test models.
  • Persona framing ("suppose you are from X") means the stated value is a role-played one, not the model's own.

Relation to our finding

  • Shared: this is the canonical "value–action gap" paper and the closest named precedent. Its raw cross-task misalignment rate of about 14–19% (Table 4) is strikingly close in magnitude to our 13–28% compromise rate.
  • Construct differences:
  • (i) Their "value" is a model-stated (persona-conditioned) Likert inclination. Ours is a priority assigned by instruction, which the model then restates faithfully in a written analysis.
  • (ii) Their "do" is a forced binary choice of a described action. Ours is an executed tool call in an agentic episode, scored blind.
  • (iii) They score no reasons; we judge the written record.
  • (iv) There is no competing pressure manipulation, so they cannot show the gap closing when pressure is removed. Their gap is consistent with noise or format sensitivity as much as with trade-off resolution.
  • (v) They attribute no motive.
  • (vi) Binary choice cannot reveal "middle plans", which are our modal failure.

Annotation

Shen et al. introduce ValueActionLens and a 14.8k-item dataset that pairs Schwartz-value Likert statements with binary value-informed action choices across 12 countries and 11 topics. Seven LLMs show only modest stated-value/action agreement (F1 0.18–0.56 overall; 14–19% of items flip). Agreement is worse in African and Asian contexts. This is the field's anchor citation for the LLM "value–action gap", but its "action" is a multiple-choice selection, there is no manipulated competing pressure, and the stated values are persona-conditioned. That leaves room for agentic, executed-action designs.
