Chiu et al. (2025) — Will AI Tell Lies to Save Sick Children? Litmus-Testing AI Values Prioritization with AIRiskDilemmas

  • Citation: Yu Ying Chiu, Zhilin Wang, Sharan Maiya, Yejin Choi, Kyle Fish, Sydney Levine, Evan Hubinger. "Will AI Tell Lies to Save Sick Children? Litmus-Testing AI Values Prioritization with AIRiskDilemmas." arXiv:2505.14633, May 2025.
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed at the version read).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14633 · data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/kellycyy/AIRiskDilemmas
  • PDF downloaded: yes — chiu2025_airiskdilemmas.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Stated preferences: binary survey-style questions between 16 shared AI values (Privacy, Care, Truthfulness, …), using five templates, e.g., "Which is more important to uphold: {v1} or {v2}?". All 16P2 pairs × 5 templates = 1,200 questions.
  • Revealed preferences: choices in AIRiskDilemmas, contextualised AI-safety-relevant dilemmas (power seeking, alignment faking, and similar) that pit values against each other.
  • Method: values are Elo-rated from aggregate choices, a pipeline called LitmusValues.

Method and models

  • Models: more than 20, including Llama, DeepSeek, Mistral, GPT and Claude families. The stated vs revealed comparison was run for GPT-4o (2024-08-06) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (§2.5).
  • Further analyses: reasoning effort (o3-mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, DeepSeek-R1 at different token budgets); prediction of HarmBench scores from revealed values.

Key quantitative findings

  • Stated preferences are stable. They are nearly identical with and without value definitions (Spearman ρ: GPT-4o 0.976; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 0.991).
  • Stated and revealed rankings diverge (§2.5, Fig. 4).
  • Spearman ρ is negative: GPT-4o −0.115; Claude 3.7 Sonnet −0.318.
  • Example: both models state Privacy at rank 14 of 16 but reveal it at rank 1.
  • Revealed preferences are more consistent than stated ones across contexts (Krippendorff's α). Claude 3.7 Sonnet: 0.762 revealed vs 0.550 stated. GPT-4o: 0.692 vs 0.629.
  • Reasoning effort barely changes revealed values (Fig. 5).
  • Revealed values predict risky behaviour, both seen in AIRiskDilemmas and unseen in HarmBench.

Authors' explanation

  • Human stated and revealed preferences diverge, and so do AI ones.
  • "Simply asking the models for their values cannot be used to effectively predict their revealed preference when making action choices in risk-prone situations."
  • Revealed preferences are argued to be more robust to selective misreporting.

Limitations

  • "Revealed" choices are still hypothetical binary text choices.
  • The value set and dilemmas are LLM-generated.
  • The stated vs revealed analysis covers two models.
  • Mahajan et al. (2026) show that the size of the SvR correlation depends on protocol (forced choice vs neutrality allowed).

Relation to our finding

  • Strongest quantitative prior for the claim that stated value rankings fail to predict choices (negative ρ).
  • Its revealed > stated consistency result suggests the action channel carries the more stable "true" priority. This mirrors our view that the tool-call action, not the analysis, is the decisive measurement.
  • Differences:
  • The stated values are the model's own abstract rankings. Ours is an instruction-assigned priority that the model restates faithfully.
  • Actions are hypothetical choices with no compromise option.
  • No reasons are judged.
  • No competing-pressure removal condition.

Annotation

Chiu et al. introduce LitmusValues and AIRiskDilemmas, which Elo-rank 16 AI values from models' choices in AI-safety dilemmas across more than 20 LLMs. Stated value rankings (survey-style pairwise questions) correlate negatively with revealed rankings (GPT-4o ρ = −0.115; Claude 3.7 Sonnet ρ = −0.318; e.g., Privacy stated 14th but revealed 1st). Revealed preferences are more internally consistent and predict HarmBench risk. A central reference for stated–revealed divergence in frontier models.
