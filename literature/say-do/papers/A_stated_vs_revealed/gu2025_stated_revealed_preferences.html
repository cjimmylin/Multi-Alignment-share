Gu, Wang & Han (2025) — Alignment Revisited: Are Large Language Models Consistent in Stated and Revealed Preferences?

  • Citation: Zhuojun Gu, Quan Wang, Shuchu Han. "Alignment Revisited: Are Large Language Models Consistent in Stated and Revealed Preferences?" arXiv:2506.00751, 31 May 2025.
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed; "Preprint. Under review").
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00751
  • PDF downloaded: yes — gu2025_stated_revealed_preferences.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Stated preference: the model's forced binary choice between two general principles in a base prompt, asked through 11 paraphrases. Example: whether it is acceptable to sacrifice one to save five, or a risk-neutral versus risk-averse principle.
  • Revealed preference: its choice in contextualised variants of the same dilemma. Examples: the autonomous-car version, probabilistic outcomes, or adding "most players have…".
  • Deviation: measured by the absolute difference in probability of the dominant principle, and by KL divergence between the contextual and prior distributions.

Method and models

  • Prompts: hand-crafted forced-choice prompts across five domains: moral dilemmas (MD), risk preference (RP), equity/fairness (EF), reciprocity (RCP), miscellaneous choice (MC), plus a few others (CP, EE).
  • Models: the abstract says "four mainstream LLMs", but Table 1 lists three: GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.0 Flash.
  • Claude excluded: Claude was dropped from the deviation analysis. It chose neutrality on about 84% of base (stated) prompts, so no dominant stated principle could be identified (§4.4).

Key quantitative findings

Overall deviation (Tables 2–3):

Model Mean absolute deviation Mean KL
GPT 0.371 0.697
Gemini 0.355 0.424

Domain contrasts: - Reciprocity (RCP): GPT absolute deviation 0.617, KL 1.306; Gemini 0.288 and 0.143. - Moral dilemmas: Gemini shifts more (0.423 vs 0.241). KL is about 0.74–0.77 for both. - Miscellaneous choice: Gemini deviation 0.632, KL 1.233.

General result: "a minor change in prompt format can often pivot the preferred choice" for all categories and models.

Authors' explanation

  • Models activate different guiding principles in context.
  • The authors note that "the model's surface-level reasoning does not necessarily reflect the true causal factor behind its decision" (§4.4).
  • They attribute Claude's neutrality to "a shallow alignment strategy designed to avoid committing to explicit principles".

Limitations

  • Prompts are manually crafted and few.
  • The analysis effectively covers two models.
  • The "revealed" condition is itself a hypothetical text answer; Wang et al. (2026, AIES) explicitly criticise this design as not truly revealed.
  • No significance testing.
  • The abstract and body disagree on model count.

Relation to our finding

  • Shared: this paper popularised the stated-vs-revealed (SvR) vocabulary for LLMs. Its key concept, context activating a different guiding principle, maps onto our "competing pressure". Its remark that surface reasoning may not reflect the causal factor parallels our judged-reasons channel.
  • Differences:
  • Their "revealed" preference is a contextual forced-choice text answer, with no action and no consequences.
  • The principle is model-stated, not instruction-assigned.
  • Deviations are measured in distribution, not per episode.
  • No compromise or middle option exists.
  • Claude's 84% neutrality is interesting relative to our "middle plan" finding. When allowed, some models decline to commit to a principle at all, an analogue of compromise at the stated level.

Annotation

Gu et al. formalise the stated–revealed preference deviation for LLMs: forced-choice answers to abstract principles versus contextualised dilemmas, quantified by absolute deviation and KL divergence. GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.0 Flash frequently flip principles under minor contextual changes (mean absolute deviation about 0.36). Claude 3.7 Sonnet refuses to state a principle on about 84% of base prompts. This is a useful early operationalisation, but the "revealed" condition remains hypothetical and only two models are analysed.
