Andric (2025) — Do Large Language Models Walk Their Talk? Measuring the Gap Between Implicit Associations, Self-Report, and Behavioral Altruism

  • Citation: Sandro Andric. "Do Large Language Models Walk Their Talk? Measuring the Gap Between Implicit Associations, Self-Report, and Behavioral Altruism." arXiv:2512.01568, Dec 2025.
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed; single author).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01568 · code: https://github.com/sandroandric/LLMs_Altruism_Study_Code
  • PDF downloaded: yes — andric2025_walk_their_talk_altruism.pdf

Say–do construct

Three measures are taken for each model: - Implicit: an LLM adaptation of the Implicit Association Test (LLM-IAT) on altruism words. - Say: a 15-item altruism self-assessment (LLM-ASA). - Do: forced binary choices between self-focused and other-focused options in 17 scenarios across 5 categories.

The "Calibration Gap" is self-reported minus behavioural altruism.

Method and models

  • Models: 24, from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, xAI, IBM, Microsoft and Z-AI.
  • Design: three repeats each; Pearson correlations; t-tests.

Key quantitative findings

  • Implicit pro-altruism bias is strong: mean IAT = 0.873, t(23) = 40.3.
  • Behaviour is altruistic above chance: 65.6% vs 50% (range 47.9% for mistral-nemo to 85.4% for claude-3.5-sonnet).
  • Implicit associations do not predict behaviour: r = .22, p = .29.
  • Self-report vs behaviour: r = .36, not significant (power .71 for r = .50).
  • Models overestimate their altruism: they claim 77.5% while acting at 65.6% (p < .0001, d = 1.08). This "virtue signalling gap" affects 75% of models.
  • Only 12.5% of models are both highly prosocial and well calibrated.
  • Methodological note (§3.3): in a pilot with three-option scenarios (Self / Balanced / Other), all models chose "Balanced" 100% of the time. The author switched to forced binary choice to remove the ceiling effect.

Authors' explanation

  • Models "know" altruism is good, and self-report reflects that normative knowledge more than their behavioural tendencies.
  • Proposes the Calibration Gap as an alignment metric.

Limitations

  • Small scenario set (17).
  • Hypothetical choices.
  • Low power for correlations.
  • Single author; preprint.

Relation to our finding

  • Directly relevant to our middle-plan finding: offered a balanced option, every model picked it every time. A compromise option is a powerful attractor for LLMs. Many binary-choice say–do benchmarks remove it by design and so cannot see the behaviour we document.
  • Self-flattering self-report is a "say > do" direction like ours.
  • Differences:
  • The "say" is a self-assessment, not an assigned priority.
  • No tool actions or judged reasons.
  • No manipulated pressure.

Annotation

Andric measures implicit associations, self-reported altruism and forced-choice altruistic behaviour in 24 LLMs. Models show strong implicit pro-altruism (IAT 0.87) and above-chance altruistic choices (65.6%), but overstate their altruism in self-report (77.5%, d = 1.08), a "virtue signalling gap". A pilot found that every model chose the "Balanced" option 100% of the time when one was offered, which bears on compromise behaviour in say–do designs. A small-scale preprint.
