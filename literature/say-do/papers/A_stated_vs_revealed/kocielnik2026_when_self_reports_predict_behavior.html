Kocielnik et al. (2026) — Rethinking Psychometric Evaluation of LLMs: When and Why Self-Reports Predict Behavior

  • Citation: Rafal Kocielnik, Pengrui Han, Peiyang Song, Myrl G. Marmarelis, Ramit Debnath, Dean Mobbs, Anima Anandkumar, R. Michael Alvarez. "Rethinking Psychometric Evaluation of LLMs: When and Why Self-Reports Predict Behavior." arXiv:2606.12730, June 2026.
  • Venue: ICML 2026 Workshop on Combining Theory and Benchmarks (CTB), oral (peer-reviewed workshop).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12730
  • PDF downloaded: yes — kocielnik2026_when_self_reports_predict_behavior.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Say: self-reports under two frameworks:
  • Big Five, a broad trait instrument;
  • the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB): intention, attitude, subjective norm and perceived behavioural control, each tied to a specific Target-Action-Context-Time (TACT).
  • Do: four tasks — Columbia Card Task risk-taking; Asch-style sycophancy; honesty; and IAT implicit bias (expected to be dissociated).
  • 2×2×2 design: framework × session context (shared thread vs separate conversations) × identity induction (parameter perturbation vs persona prompting).

Method and models

  • Models: 11 frontier LLMs.
  • Benchmarks: compared with human meta-analytic intention–behaviour validity (r ≈ .47) and trait–behaviour validity (r ≈ .20).

Key quantitative findings

  • Within a shared conversation, TPB reaches human-level coherence.
  • Pooled SR–behaviour r = +0.25 (95% CI 0.22–0.28).
  • Excluding the IAT task, r = +0.40 (0.37–0.43), within the human range of 0.25–0.50.
  • 41 of 77 cells (53.2%) are theory-aligned and significant, 21.3× the chance rate.
  • Big Five stays near zero.
  • Across separate conversations, coherence survives only for behaviours anchored outside the prompt, such as training-shaped implicit bias.
  • Coherence collapses for context-primed behaviour such as sycophancy. Several models (Qwen3-235B, Gemini 2.5 Flash, DeepSeek V3.1) show near-complete behavioural reversal across sessions (behaviour consistency r ≈ −0.9).
  • IAT bias is inverted. Models reporting higher intention to be unbiased produce more stereotype-consistent responses, like compensatory-effort inversions in humans.
  • Persona prompting makes self-reports more consistent across conversations but does not bring behaviour into line.

Authors' explanation

  • Self-report–behaviour coherence is selective.
  • It needs behaviour-specific instruments and a shared context.
  • It fails when behaviour is strongly primed by the immediate context.

Limitations

  • Workshop paper. Four tasks.
  • Within-session coherence could partly reflect consistency pressure (the model echoing its prior stated intention).

Relation to our finding

  • Highly relevant boundary condition: stated intentions do predict behaviour when (a) the statement is behaviour-specific and (b) it sits in the same context. Our analyses meet both conditions: they are task-specific and written in-episode.
  • Yet the gap still appears in 13–28% of episodes, under competing pressure. This fits their finding that coherence collapses "when behavior is strongly primed by context". Competing pressure is just such a contextual prime.
  • Differences: personality and behavioural tasks rather than value priorities, no tool actions, and no judged reasons.

Annotation

Kocielnik et al. revisit the LLM self-report/behaviour dissociation with a Theory of Planned Behavior instrument and a shared vs separate session manipulation across 11 frontier LLMs. Behaviour-specific intentions in the same conversation predict behaviour at human-level validity (r ≈ .40 excluding IAT); Big Five does not. Coherence collapses across sessions for context-primed behaviours like sycophancy. Persona prompts stabilise self-report but not behaviour. Essential for specifying when "say" should predict "do".
