Moore, Deshpande & Yang (2024) — Are Large Language Models Consistent over Value-laden Questions?

  • Citation: Jared Moore, Tanvi Deshpande, Diyi Yang. "Are Large Language Models Consistent over Value-laden Questions?" Findings of EMNLP 2024. arXiv:2407.02996.
  • Venue: Findings of EMNLP 2024 (peer-reviewed).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02996
  • PDF downloaded: yes — moore2024_value_laden_consistency.pdf

Say–do construct

Value consistency is defined as similarity of answers across four things: 1. paraphrases of one question; 2. related questions within one topic; 3. use-cases: multiple-choice vs open-ended answers to the same question (the closest to say vs do); 4. translations (English, Chinese, German, Japanese).

Method and models

  • Dataset: VALUECONSISTENCY, about 8,000 GPT-4-generated questions on more than 300 topics, for the U.S., China, Germany and Japan.
  • Models: base and chat models (llama-2/llama-3 7B–70B, cmd-r, yi-34b, gpt-4o).
  • Measurement: stance distributions compared via a Jensen-Shannon-based measure. Multiple-choice uses option log-probabilities; open-ended stance is classified by llama3.
  • Human comparison: participants (n = 165 total; about 81–84 per consistency measure).
  • Steerability test: can models be steered to Schwartz values?

Key quantitative findings

  • Large models (≥ 34B) are relatively consistent across paraphrases, use-cases, translations and within topics, and on par with humans for topic and paraphrase consistency (Fig. 4).
  • Topic type matters. Models are less consistent on controversial topics ("euthanasia") than uncontroversial ones ("Thanksgiving") (Fig. 5).
  • Base vs fine-tuned. Base models are more consistent, and uniformly so. Fine-tuned models are inconsistent on some topics (euthanasia, religious freedom) and consistent on others (women's rights), a pattern like humans' (Fig. 3, Fig. 6).
  • "Models are not steerable to Schwartz values" (§6.5, Fig. 9).

Authors' explanation

  • Consistency is a necessary but not sufficient condition for having values.
  • Fine-tuning introduces topic-specific inconsistency.
  • The authors argue against prior claims of pervasive inconsistency (contra Röttger et al., 2024), attributing the difference to measurement choices.

Limitations

  • Four languages. GPT-4-generated questions.
  • The open-ended stance is judged by an LLM.
  • "Use-case" consistency is still about opinions in text, not actions.

Relation to our finding

  • Important counterweight: at the level of verbal stance across formats, large models can be fairly consistent. This suggests the say–do gaps found elsewhere are not just format noise. A gap that appears specifically when an action under competing pressure is required (as in our design) is a different phenomenon from verbal inconsistency.
  • Non-steerability to Schwartz values parallels evidence that instructions change words more than choices.
  • Differences: no actions and no assigned priority.

Annotation

Moore et al. define value consistency over paraphrases, topics, multiple-choice vs open-ended use-cases, and languages, using an 8k-question, 300-topic dataset. Large LLMs prove about as consistent as human participants, though less so on controversial topics. Fine-tuned models are more topic-selectively inconsistent than base models. Models also resist steering toward specified Schwartz values. A useful corrective showing that verbal value inconsistency is smaller than some prior work claimed, which isolates action-level gaps as a distinct issue.
