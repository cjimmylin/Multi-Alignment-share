Chiu, Jiang & Choi (2024/2025) — DailyDilemmas: Revealing Value Preferences of LLMs with Quandaries of Daily Life

  • Citation: Yu Ying Chiu, Liwei Jiang, Yejin Choi. "DailyDilemmas: Revealing Value Preferences of LLMs with Quandaries of Daily Life." ICLR 2025 (spotlight). arXiv:2410.02683.
  • Venue: ICLR 2025 (peer-reviewed, spotlight).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02683
  • PDF downloaded: yes — chiu2024_dailydilemmas.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Revealed: value preferences inferred from the action an LLM chooses in 1,360 everyday two-option moral dilemmas. Each option is annotated with affected parties and the values it serves.
  • Developer-level stated principles vs revealed behaviour (§5): the published principles of OpenAI's ModelSpec and Anthropic's Constitution are mapped to supporting and opposing value groups, then compared with the models' revealed value choices.
  • Steering test: system prompts stating which values to prioritise, to see whether choices change.

Method and models

  • Dataset: dilemmas generated by GPT-4. Values mapped onto five frameworks: World Values Survey, Moral Foundations Theory, Maslow, Aristotle's virtues, Plutchik's emotions.
  • Models: a range of LLMs, including GPT-4-turbo and Mixtral-8x7B (named in the abstract).
  • Case studies: GPT-4-turbo (ModelSpec) and Claude-3-Haiku (Constitution).
  • Steering experiment: GPT-4-turbo with ModelSpec-derived prompts (Table 14).

Key quantitative findings

  • Overall leanings: models lean toward self-expression over survival (WVS) and care over loyalty (MFT).
  • Model differences on truthfulness: Mixtral-8x7B neglects it by 9.7%; GPT-4-turbo selects it by 9.4% (abstract).
  • Principle vs behaviour (Table 1, §5):
  • GPT-4-turbo, despite ModelSpec's "protect people's privacy", favours transparency over privacy and respect. The weighted score difference is −1.5.
  • Claude-3-Haiku aligns more closely with "reducing existential risk", prioritising safety and caution over freedom and innovation.
  • System-prompt steering of GPT-4-turbo is "ineffective in general" (§5.3, Fig. 5). Under some principles, both the pro and the anti steering prompts moved preferences in the same direction.

Authors' explanation

  • Published principles conceal implicit value conflicts.
  • Actual prioritisation emerges from training, and end users "cannot effectively steer such prioritization using system prompts".

Limitations

  • Binary choices in hypothetical dilemmas.
  • GPT-4-generated scenarios and value labels.
  • Mapping principles to values involves judgement.
  • Steering is tested on one model only.

Relation to our finding

  • Two relevant elements: 1. Developer-stated principles vs model-revealed choices. This is an institution-level say–do gap. 2. A system prompt that states a priority does not reliably move choices. That is very close to our instruction-assigned priority.
  • Differences:
  • Choices are hypothetical and binary, so there is no middle plan.
  • No analysis written by the model is scored.
  • No manipulated competing pressure.
  • The "say" is the developer's document, not the model's own text.

Annotation

DailyDilemmas offers 1,360 everyday two-option dilemmas whose chosen actions reveal LLM value priorities, analysed across five value frameworks. It compares revealed priorities with OpenAI's ModelSpec and Anthropic's Constitution, finding mixed adherence (e.g., GPT-4-turbo favours transparency over its privacy principle). System-prompt steering of GPT-4-turbo's value trade-offs is largely ineffective. A key ICLR benchmark for revealed value preferences and for the weakness of prompt-stated priorities.
