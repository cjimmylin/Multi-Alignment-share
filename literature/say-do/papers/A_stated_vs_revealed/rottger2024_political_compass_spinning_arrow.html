Röttger et al. (2024) — Political Compass or Spinning Arrow? Towards More Meaningful Evaluations for Values and Opinions in Large Language Models

  • Citation: Paul Röttger, Valentin Hofmann, Valentina Pyatkin, Musashi Hinck, Hannah Rose Kirk, Hinrich Schütze, Dirk Hovy. "Political Compass or Spinning Arrow? Towards More Meaningful Evaluations for Values and Opinions in Large Language Models." Proceedings of ACL 2024 (Main). arXiv:2402.16786.
  • Venue: ACL 2024 Main Conference (peer-reviewed).
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16786 · ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.816/
  • PDF downloaded: yes — rottger2024_political_compass_spinning_arrow.pdf

Say–do construct

  • Constrained "say": a multiple-choice survey answer on the Political Compass Test (PCT).
  • Realistic "do": an open-ended generation (e.g., writing a blog post or op-ed giving its opinion on the same proposition), classified agree/disagree.
  • The gap is between survey-format value expression and downstream-like generation.

Method and models

  • Systematic review: 12 prior PCT-based LLM papers. Most force a multiple-choice format.
  • Models: up to 10 LLMs (GPT-3.5/4 variants, Llama-2 7/13/70B, Mistral 7B Instruct v0.1/v0.2, Zephyr). All 62 PCT propositions.
  • Experiments:
  • unforced multiple-choice;
  • five forced-choice prompts of increasing force;
  • paraphrase robustness (10 template paraphrases);
  • open-ended setting with 10 templates (Mistral 7B Iv0.1, GPT-3.5 1106). GPT-4 classifier: 99–100% accuracy against human labels; κ = 0.931.

Key quantitative findings

  • Unforced, most models give invalid answers. Llama-2-7b gives only 6.5% valid; Mistral Iv0.1 75.8%. In 95% of annotated invalid responses the model says it has no opinion (§4.2).
  • Forcing works unevenly. GPT-4 models are "essentially immune" to forcing; GPT-3.5 gives ≥ 80.6% valid; Mistral 7b Iv0.1 gives 100% (§4.3).
  • Paraphrases move compass positions by more than the Biden–Trump distance.
  • Mistral moves from (−3.6, −5.2) to (−6.0, −3.5).
  • GPT-3.5 moves from (−1.5, −1.9) to (−3.2, −4.4).
  • Contradictions appear on 14 of 62 (Mistral) and 23 of 62 (GPT-3.5) propositions (§4.4).
  • Open-ended vs multiple-choice (§4.5, Fig. 4):
  • On about one in three propositions (19/62 GPT-3.5; 23/62 Mistral), models agree in multiple-choice but disagree in open-ended generation.
  • There is no reverse flip.
  • Changes skew right-leaning and libertarian: 66.6% (GPT-3.5) and 70.0% (Mistral) of economic changes go left→right.

Authors' explanation

  • Constrained survey evaluations are artificial. Models often "do not have" a single opinion, and forcing one manufactures it.
  • The authors recommend evaluating in realistic, unconstrained settings that match the use case, and making robustness claims "local".

Limitations

  • Political opinions only, using the PCT.
  • The open-ended analysis covers two models.
  • Open-ended output is still text, not action.
  • Some flips may reflect genre conventions (e.g., persuasive writing).

Relation to our finding

  • Shared: a foundational ACL demonstration that a model's survey answer does not predict its downstream generation. The direction of the shift is systematic, not random, which supports our claim that the "do" channel should be measured directly.
  • Differences:
  • The "say" is a model's own opinion under forced choice, not an instruction-assigned priority restated in analysis.
  • The "do" is free text, not tool calls.
  • No competing pressure is manipulated, and no reasons are judged.
  • The "unforced → invalid / both sides" behaviour (63% of invalid responses argue both sides) parallels middle-plan or compromise behaviour when a model is not forced to commit.

Annotation

Röttger et al. review PCT-based LLM evaluations and show that forced multiple-choice answers are artefacts. Unforced models mostly decline; GPT-4 resists forcing; paraphrases move compass positions further than the Biden–Trump distance. In open-ended writing, models reverse their multiple-choice stance on about a third of propositions. It is the standard citation for "survey answers ≠ downstream behaviour" in LLM values and opinions work, and it argues for use-case-realistic evaluation.
