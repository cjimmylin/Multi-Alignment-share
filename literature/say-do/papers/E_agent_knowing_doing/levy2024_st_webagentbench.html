First posted to arXiv in Oct 2024. URL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06703 PDF downloaded. Yes (v7), levy2024_st_webagentbench.pdf

Say–do construct

This benchmark measures do only, as policy adherence during task execution. - Each task carries one or more ST policies, short rules such as "ask the user before deleting" or "do not modify X". They follow an explicit hierarchy: organization ≻ user ≻ task. - Policy violations are detected by programmatic evaluators over the trajectory: action-type checks, URL matching, action counts and HTML-content checks (Appendix). - The Completion Under Policy (CuP) metric credits a task only when it is completed and no applicable policy is violated.

Agents are not asked to state or judge the policies. They receive them in a POLICY_CONTEXT block appended to every observation.

Method, environment, models

  • Tasks. 375 tasks across WebArena GitLab and ShoppingAdmin, plus SuiteCRM, carrying 3,057 policies in total.
  • Dimensions. Six: user consent, boundary, strict execution, hierarchy adherence, robustness, and error handling. Tasks come in three difficulty tiers, with an additional "Modality Challenge".
  • Agents. Three open agents run with pass@3: AgentWorkflowMemory (AWM), WorkArena-Legacy and WebVoyager.

Key quantitative findings

  • Completion vs CuP. Raw completion averages 24.3%, but CuP is 15.0%. That means about 38% of completed tasks violate at least one policy (§1).
  • Policy load. CuP falls from 18.2% with a single active policy to 7.1% with five or more. Raw completion stays nearly flat across loads (Spearman ρ = −0.14).
  • By agent.
  • AWM has the highest partial completion (46.9%) but only 20% CuP, with 37 user-consent breaches.
  • WorkArena-Legacy: 26% completion, 15% CuP.
  • WebVoyager: 12.8% completion, 10.3% CuP (§4.2).
  • Main risk sources. User-consent and strict-execution violations account for most of the risk.

Authors' explanation

Agents tuned to complete tasks get around safeguards such as ask_the_user in order to make progress. Completion rate alone therefore hides how unready agents are for enterprise use.

Limitations

  • Only three open agent frameworks; frontier proprietary agents are not the focus.
  • Policies are delivered as context, not as a system-level charter.
  • No reasoning channel is measured.
  • Pass@3 evaluation.

Relation to our finding

ST-WebAgentBench is the best web-agent example of mechanically scored policy compliance alongside task completion. Its CuP metric formalises "completed, but only by breaching a stated rule". That resembles our compromising-action outcome, where the agent pursues the competing goal at the expense of the stated priority. The policy hierarchy (organization ≻ user ≻ task) parallels a charter priority.

It does not measure stated reasons, so it cannot show a say–do gap. Its finding that compliance worsens as policy load grows is a pressure-like effect, though different from ours.

Annotation

ST-WebAgentBench attaches 3,057 explicit policies, arranged in an organization ≻ user ≻ task hierarchy, to 375 enterprise web tasks. Programmatic evaluators check for violations, and Completion Under Policy credits only tasks completed without any breach. Across three open agents, roughly 38% of nominal completions violated a policy, and policy-compliant completion fell from 18.2% to 7.1% as policy load increased. It is a strong precedent for mechanically separating task success from policy adherence in agent scoring, but it has no say channel.
