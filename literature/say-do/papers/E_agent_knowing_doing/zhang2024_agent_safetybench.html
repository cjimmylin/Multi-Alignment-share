Zhang et al. (2024): Agent-SafetyBench: Evaluating the Safety of LLM Agents

Citation. Zhang, Z., Cui, S., Lu, Y., Zhou, J., Yang, J., Wang, H., & Huang, M. (2024). Agent-SafetyBench: Evaluating the Safety of LLM Agents. arXiv:2412.14470 (v2, May 2025). Tsinghua CoAI. Venue. Preprint ("Preprint. Under review." on v2). I have not verified any later venue. URL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14470 · https://github.com/thu-coai/Agent-SafetyBench/ PDF downloaded. Yes, zhang2024_agent_safetybench.pdf.

Say–do construct

The benchmark measures act mode. Agents interact with simulated environments, and a fine-tuned LLM scorer then labels the full interaction record as safe or unsafe. The scorer reaches 91.5% accuracy on a held-out set, against roughly 15 points less for GPT-4o used directly (§3).

There is no separate judge-mode probe. Instead, the authors diagnose a "lack of risk awareness" from behaviour: agents often call tools with correct parameters while overlooking the obvious risks, for example disabling every alarm system (§4.3). Content-safety cases are scored separately from behaviour-safety cases.

Method, environment, models

  • Scale: 349 interactive environments and 2,000 test cases.
  • Coverage: 8 risk categories and 10 failure modes (M1–M10). Each case is also annotated as fulfillable (can be completed safely) or unfulfillable.
  • Models: 16 agents, including Claude-3-Opus, Claude-3.5-Sonnet and Haiku, GPT-4o, GPT-4-Turbo, Gemini-1.5-Pro and Flash, Qwen2.5, Llama3.1, DeepSeek-V2.5 and GLM4.
  • Defences: two defence prompts, one simple and one enhanced, each listing the failure modes.

Key quantitative findings

  • No agent scores above 60% safety (Table 5):
  • Claude-3-Opus 59.8% and Claude-3.5-Sonnet 59.4% are the best.
  • GPT-4o scores 44.2%.
  • Llama3.1-8B (19.9%) and Qwen2.5-7B (18.8%) are the worst.
  • Behaviour vs content: agents do worse on behaviour safety than on content safety, even though most behaviour cases contain no jailbreak (§4.2).
  • "Spread" category: averages only 15.6%. Agents post or email unverified unsafe information.
  • Fulfillable vs unfulfillable: agents are less safe on unfulfillable cases, where the task cannot be done safely, than on fulfillable ones (Fig. 3).
  • Defence prompts listing the failure modes give only limited improvement and are judged "ineffective" (§4.5, Fig. 4).

Authors' explanation

The authors identify two defects: (1) lack of robustness, meaning incorrect tool use, and (2) lack of risk awareness, meaning the agent overlooks consequences. Prompting alone cannot fix either.

Limitations

  • The scorer is a fine-tuned LLM.
  • Environments and tools are simulated.
  • There is no direct test of whether agents know the risk when asked, so "lack of awareness" is inferred from behaviour.
  • The paper was under review at the time of reading.

Relation to our finding

Agent-SafetyBench shows how hard act-mode safety is across a broad set of cases. Its "unfulfillable" category, where safe completion is impossible, is structurally close to our competing-pressure condition: agents become less safe when the task and safety cannot both be satisfied. That matches our finding that the gap opens under competing pressure.

Its "lack of risk awareness" diagnosis differs from ours in an important way. Our agents demonstrably articulate the priority and still compromise, so awareness is not what is missing. This paper never measured awareness directly, and the knowing–doing papers (Tang 2025; PropensityBench) suggest that awareness is often present. The action is judged by an LLM scorer over the whole record, so actions are not scored blind to reasons.

Annotation

Agent-SafetyBench evaluates 16 LLM agents on 2,000 cases across 349 simulated tool environments, using a fine-tuned safety scorer (91.5% accuracy). No agent exceeds 60% safety. Agents are less safe when a task cannot be completed safely, and defence prompts barely help. The authors attribute failures to poor robustness and a "lack of risk awareness", an inference from behaviour that later knowing–doing studies dispute.
