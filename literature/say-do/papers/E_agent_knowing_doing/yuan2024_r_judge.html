Yuan et al. (2024): R-Judge: Benchmarking Safety Risk Awareness for LLM Agents

Citation. Yuan, T., He, Z., Dong, L., Wang, Y., Zhao, R., Xia, T., Xu, L., Zhou, B., Li, F., Zhang, Z., Wang, R., & Liu, G. (2024). R-Judge: Benchmarking Safety Risk Awareness for LLM Agents. Findings of EMNLP 2024. arXiv:2401.10019. Venue. Findings of EMNLP 2024 (peer-reviewed). URL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10019 · https://github.com/Lordog/R-Judge PDF downloaded. Yes: yuan2024_r_judge.pdf.

Say–do construct

R-Judge measures judge mode only. An LLM reads a recorded multi-turn agent interaction (user instruction, agent actions, environment feedback) and must do two things: 1. Write a risk identification analysis. A GPT-4 scorer compares this with a human-written risk description. 2. Output a binary safety judgment (safe or unsafe), scored with F1.

The model judges trajectories that other agents produced. It is never asked to act, so R-Judge measures "know/recognise" only. Paired with act-mode benchmarks, it supplies the judge-mode half of a judge-vs-act comparison.

Method, environment, models

  • Dataset. 569 human-annotated records covering 27 scenarios in 5 categories (program, IoT, software, web, finance) and 10 risk types, ranging from privacy leakage to ethics and morality.
  • Sources. Records are drawn partly from ToolEmu, InjecAgent and AgentMonitor data and partly built by the authors. Some unsafe ToolEmu cases were transformed into safe ones to balance the labels (§3).
  • Models. 11 LLMs: the GPT series (including GPT-4o), Llama-2 and Llama-3, Mistral, and Vicuna.

Key quantitative findings

  • Safety judgment. The best model, GPT-4o, reaches F1 = 74.45%. In the authors' words, "no other models significantly exceed the random" baseline, which scores about 51 F1 (Table 1, Abstract).
  • Prompting vs fine-tuning. Fine-tuning on safety judgment improves performance substantially. Straightforward prompting mechanisms do not (§5).
  • What drives difficulty. Risk awareness in open agent scenarios is multi-dimensional, requiring knowledge, reasoning, and understanding of the scenario.

Authors' explanation

Agent-scenario risk judgment demands contextual reasoning about how actions interact with the environment. This differs from content-harm detection, which is what most safety training targets.

Limitations

  • Modest dataset size (569 records).
  • Binary labels.
  • Risk-identification scoring relies on GPT-4.
  • No act-mode counterpart, so judging and acting are never compared within the same models.

Relation to our finding

R-Judge is the standard judge-mode benchmark. Its central result is that, in 2024, even recognising risk in concrete trajectories was hard: only GPT-4o clearly beat chance. That contrasts with Tang et al. (2025) and PrivacyLens, where abstract knowledge is near ceiling, and fits Tang's "knowledge–identification gap".

For our paper, R-Judge is a reminder that "know" has levels: - abstract principle, - recognition in concrete context, - self-application.

Our agents' written analyses show they articulate the priority correctly in context. Their failure therefore lies at the identification→execution step, not at recognition. R-Judge itself has no act channel.

Annotation

R-Judge asks LLMs to act as judges of 569 recorded agent trajectories across 27 scenarios. Each model writes a risk analysis and gives a safe/unsafe verdict. Only GPT-4o (F1 74.45%) clearly beat random, and fine-tuning helped more than prompting. It is the canonical judge-mode agent-safety benchmark and the natural counterpart to act-mode benchmarks, but on its own it does not measure whether the judging model would act consistently with its verdicts.
