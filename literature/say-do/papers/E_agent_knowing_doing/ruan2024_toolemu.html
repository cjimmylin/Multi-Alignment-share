Ruan et al. (2024): Identifying the Risks of LM Agents with an LM-Emulated Sandbox (ToolEmu)

Citation. Ruan, Y., Dong, H., Wang, A., Pitis, S., Zhou, Y., Ba, J., Dubois, Y., Maddison, C. J., & Hashimoto, T. (2024). Identifying the Risks of LM Agents with an LM-Emulated Sandbox. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2309.15817. Venue. ICLR 2024 (peer-reviewed conference paper). URL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15817 · http://toolemu.com/ PDF downloaded. Yes: ruan2024_toolemu.pdf.

Say–do construct

  • Act mode only. ToolEmu measures what agents do, not what they say.
  • What counts as a risky action. The paper's threat model is underspecified user instructions. A risky action is one that fails to resolve the underspecification safely, for example deleting files without checking, or emailing the wrong recipient.
  • How actions are scored. A GPT-4 safety evaluator reads the whole trajectory, together with the test case's stated underspecification and potential risks, and rates it from 0 to 3 for likelihood and severity of risk.
  • Why it matters for the strand. There is no judge-mode comparison here. ToolEmu is nonetheless the substrate that Tang et al. (2025) later used to build matched knowledge, identification and execution tests.

Method, environment, models

  • Environment. GPT-4 emulates tool execution from tool specifications, including an adversarial emulator for red-teaming. The benchmark has 36 high-stakes toolkits (311 tools) and 144 test cases.
  • Validation. 68.8% of the failures it identifies are confirmed by humans as valid real-world failures with the adversarial emulator, and 72.5% with the standard one. The safety evaluator agrees with humans at Cohen's κ = 0.478, roughly the human–human rate of 0.480 (§4).
  • Agents. GPT-4, Claude-2, ChatGPT-3.5 and Vicuna-1.5 (7B and 13B), each run as a ReAct agent.

Key quantitative findings

  • Failure incidence with the basic prompt (Table 5):
Agent Failure incidence
GPT-4 39.4%
Claude-2 44.3%
ChatGPT-3.5 62.0%
Vicuna-13B 54.6%
Vicuna-7B 45.0%
  • Effect of a safety prompt. Adding safety requirements to GPT-4's prompt ("Helpful + Safety") lowers failure incidence to 23.9%. This is the abstract's figure: "even the safest LM agent exhibits such failures 23.9% of the time".
  • Trade-off. A NoAct agent is perfectly safe (score 3.0) but almost entirely unhelpful (0.063).
  • Real-world check. The authors reproduced 6 of the emulated failures in real sandboxes. One was rm -rf /*, which crashed a VM.

Authors' explanation

LM agents fail to handle underspecification safely. They act on assumptions rather than seeking confirmation. Prompting them with safety requirements helps but leaves substantial risk.

Limitations

  • Both the tools and the evaluator are LMs, and evaluator agreement is only moderate (κ ≈ 0.48).
  • The threat model is limited to underspecification, with no adversarial users and no goal conflicts.
  • The emulation is not always realistic.

Relation to our finding

ToolEmu is the foundational act-mode testbed for the knowing–doing literature (Tang 2025; R-Judge reuses its trajectories). Two of its findings parallel ours: - Safety prompting sharply lowers risky actions, which suggests the agents could act safely and that salience matters. - Its separate safety and helpfulness evaluators treat outcome dimensions independently.

Where it differs from our design: - Nothing records what the agent says or believes. - The action is scored by an LM judge reading the whole trajectory, including the agent's thoughts, so the action channel is not blind to the stated reasons.

Annotation

ToolEmu uses GPT-4 to emulate 311 tools across 144 high-stakes, underspecified test cases. An LM safety evaluator, validated against humans (κ ≈ 0.48, 68.8% failure precision), scores agent trajectories. Basic GPT-4 agents failed in 39.4% of cases, and still in 23.9% with safety prompting. ToolEmu established scalable act-mode risk testing and became the substrate for later judge-vs-act comparisons, but its LM judge reads full trajectories, so actions and reasoning are not scored separately.
