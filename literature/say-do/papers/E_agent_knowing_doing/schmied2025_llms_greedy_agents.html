Schmied et al. (2025): LLMs are Greedy Agents: Effects of RL Fine-tuning on Decision-Making Abilities

Citation. Schmied, T., Bornschein, J., Grau-Moya, J., Wulfmeier, M., & Pascanu, R. (2025). LLMs are Greedy Agents: Effects of RL Fine-tuning on Decision-Making Abilities. arXiv:2504.16078. Google DeepMind and JKU Linz. Venue. Preprint (arXiv v1, 22 Apr 2025). I did not verify whether it was later published at a venue. URL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16078 PDF downloaded. Yes, schmied2025_llms_greedy_agents.pdf

Say–do construct

This is a competence knowing–doing gap, not a moral or safety one. - Know: the model's own chain-of-thought rationale correctly computes the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) values and identifies the arm with the highest UCB. - Do: the action the model then selects, classed as "optimal" (highest UCB), "greedy" (best arm among those tried so far) or "other".

The gap is the share of cases with a correct rationale followed by a non-optimal action. Both channels come from the same episode.

Method, environment, models

  • Environments: multi-armed bandits, contextual bandits and text Tic-tac-toe.
  • Models: Gemma2 2B, 9B and 27B.
  • Knowing–doing analysis: Gemma2 27B is prompted to produce UCB, compute the quantities and act on them, over 64 instances × 50 steps with 2048 reasoning tokens per step (§4.2, App. C.1.3).
  • Mitigation: RL fine-tuning (RLFT) on self-generated chain-of-thought rationales, using environment reward.

Key quantitative findings

  • Knowing–doing confusion matrix (Fig. 5):
  • 87% of rationales are correct.
  • Among all actions, those with correct rationales split into 58.1% greedy, 21.6% optimal and 10.6% other.
  • So 64% of actions taken after a correct rationale are greedy rather than optimal (§1).
  • Greediness: LLMs leave up to 55% of the action space unexplored.
  • Frequency bias: Gemma2 2B copies frequent actions (96% F_f), while 27B mostly escapes this (14%).
  • RLFT: reduces regret and narrows the knowing–doing gap, but exploration remains suboptimal (§4.3).

Authors' explanation

  • Models commit early to greedy strategies.
  • Frequency bias is an artifact of supervised pre-training (cf. copycat bias).
  • RL on the model's own rationales helps couple reasoning to action.

Limitations

  • Only the Gemma2 family, at small-to-medium scale.
  • Short horizons (50 steps).
  • Toy decision tasks.
  • No safety, normative or value content.
  • "Knowing" is defined by correct arithmetic in the rationale.

Relation to our finding

This paper supplies the term "knowing–doing gap" and an operationalisation that sits within a single episode. The model's own correct written reasoning is followed by an inconsistent action, which matches our structure (written analysis faithful to the priority, then a compromising action) more closely than separate-probe designs do. Action is scored mechanically, as in our study.

It differs from our work in three ways: - Construct: the gap concerns exploration competence, not value priorities, and involves no competing social or pressure demands. - Pull: the competing pull is the model's own greedy bias rather than an external incentive. - Scoring: there is no blind judge of the reasons; correctness is computed directly.

It is useful for framing: say–do gaps are not peculiar to ethics but appear as a general decoupling of LLM reasoning from action.

Annotation

Schmied et al. measure a knowing–doing gap within single episodes for Gemma2 agents on bandit tasks. In 87% of steps the model's own chain-of-thought correctly computes the UCB-optimal arm, yet it picks the greedy arm in 64% of those cases and the optimal one in only about 21% of all steps. RL fine-tuning on self-generated rationales narrows the gap. This is the canonical non-moral demonstration that correct written reasoning need not govern LLM action, and the source of the "knowing–doing gap" label in agent research.
