Huang, Pi & Mougan (2024): Moral Persuasion in Large Language Models — Evaluating Susceptibility and Ethical Alignment

  • Citation: Huang, A., Pi, Y. N., & Mougan, C. (2024). Moral Persuasion in Large Language Models: Evaluating Susceptibility and Ethical Alignment. arXiv:2411.11731.
  • Venue: Workshop paper, AdvML-Frontiers'24 (3rd Workshop on New Frontiers in Adversarial Machine Learning @ NeurIPS 2024), per the PDF footer. Treat as lightly reviewed, not archival.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11731 (code: github.com/acyhuang/moral-persuasion)
  • PDF downloaded: yes (huang2024_moral_persuasion.pdf, 10 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: theory-grounded scenarios (MoralChoice, Scherrer et al. 2023) plus psych-scale (MFQ-30) for experiment 2

Say–do construct

  • "Say": the Base Agent's initial choice in a MoralChoice scenario, scored across six question forms.
  • "Do": its choice after a multi-turn conversation with a Persuader Agent LLM told to make it switch actions.
  • Metrics: change in action likelihood (CAL), decision change rate (DCR) and rule-violation rate (RVR).
  • Experiment 2 is separate: prompting models to adopt an ethical framework (e.g., utilitarian, deontological) and measuring the shift in MFQ-30 scores.

Method and models

  • Eight LLMs as base and persuader, including mistral-7b-instruct, llama-3.1-8b and 70b, gpt-4o-mini, gpt-4o, claude-3-haiku and claude-3.5-sonnet.
  • Conversations of 2–10 turns.
  • High-ambiguity and low-ambiguity scenario sets.

Key quantitative findings

  • Longer conversations slightly increase CAL and DCR, except for mistral-7b, whose agents drift out of role (§3.2.1, Fig. 1).
  • claude-3-haiku and llama-3.1-8b are most susceptible: "in aggregate, these two models change their original actions in almost half of the scenarios" (§3.2.2).
  • Other models (e.g., gpt-4o, claude-3.5-sonnet) mostly keep their decisions.
  • Persuasion is "largely ineffective" on low-ambiguity scenarios (§3.2.2).
  • "A model's susceptibility to persuasion is far more variable than a model's ability to persuade."
  • Persuasion raises rule violations; the largest increase is in "Do not break promise" for claude-3-haiku and llama-3.1-70b (§3.2.2).
  • Framework prompting shifts MFQ profiles for gpt-4o, claude-3-haiku and mistral-7b; other models refused (§4).

Authors' explanation

Susceptibility depends on the model, scenario ambiguity and conversation length. Models of different sizes from the same company behave very differently.

Limitations

  • Workshop-scale study.
  • LLM persuaders rather than humans.
  • Ambiguous scenarios have no correct answer, so a "change" is not necessarily an error.
  • The MFQ is a descriptive instrument, not a normative theory; experiment 2 measures instrument shifts, not moral reasoning.

Relation to our finding

This is the closest moral-domain analogue to our capitulation result: multi-turn persuasion moves moral choices in up to ~half of scenarios for susceptible models. But the persuader supplies arguments, and the scenarios are ambiguous, so updating may be legitimate. Persuasion fails on low-ambiguity items. Our design isolates content-free pushback against a faithful analysis and adds a new-fact arm, so it can show capitulation that is not explained by argument quality or real ambiguity.

Annotation

Uses LLM-vs-LLM multi-turn persuasion on MoralChoice scenarios. Weaker or smaller models (claude-3-haiku, llama-3.1-8b) change moral decisions in nearly half of high-ambiguity cases, but persuasion rarely works on low-ambiguity ones. Ethical-framework prompts shift MFQ profiles. A workshop-level reference for moral judgments shifting under conversational pressure.
