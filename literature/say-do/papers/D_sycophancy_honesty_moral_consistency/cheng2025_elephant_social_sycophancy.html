Cheng et al. (2025): ELEPHANT — Measuring and Understanding Social Sycophancy in LLMs

  • Citation: Cheng, M., Yu, S., Lee, C., Khadpe, P., Ibrahim, L., & Jurafsky, D. (2025). ELEPHANT: Measuring and understanding social sycophancy in LLMs. arXiv:2505.13995 (v2, Sep 2025).
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed; the PDF header reads "Preprint"). Stanford, CMU and Oxford.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13995
  • PDF downloaded: yes (cheng2025_elephant_social_sycophancy.pdf, 34 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: crowdsourced-folk (r/AmITheAsshole consensus, crowdsourced advice) plus ad hoc LLM scorers

Say–do construct

  • Sycophancy is reframed as face preservation (Goffman): validating the user, being indirect, accepting the user's framing, and moral sycophancy.
  • For moral sycophancy, the "stated" moral judgment is the verdict given to one party in a conflict. The "do" is the verdict given to the other party telling the same story from their side (AITA-NTA-FLIP, 1,591 pairs).
  • Consistency requires endorsing only one side.

Method and models

  • 11 models, including GPT-4o, Gemini and Llama.
  • Four datasets: open-ended advice queries; AITA-YTA (2,000 posts whose consensus is that the poster was at fault); assumption-laden statements; AITA-NTA-FLIP.
  • Responses are compared with crowdsourced human ones.
  • Preference datasets are analysed, and mitigations tested: third-person rewrite, DPO steering, truthfulness-tuned models.

Key quantitative findings

  • On advice queries LLMs validate users 50 pp more than humans (72% vs 22%), avoid direct guidance 43 pp more (66% vs 21%) and accept framing 28 pp more (88% vs 60%) (§1).
  • On AITA-YTA they preserve face 46 pp more than humans. They fail to challenge ungrounded assumptions in 86% of cases (§1). The abstract gives 45 pp averaged over the general-advice and AITA sets.
  • Moral sycophancy: LLMs affirm both sides of a moral conflict, depending on which side the user takes, in 48% of cases. Humans endorse only one side (abstract, §1).
  • Preference datasets reward social sycophancy (§4.2, Fig. 2). Mitigations have mixed effect; model-based (DPO) steering is the most promising (§1).

Authors' explanation

Post-training preference data reward validation and face preservation. Advice and support is the most common LLM use case, so implicit-belief sycophancy is the dominant real-world form.

Limitations

  • English only (§Limitations).
  • Reddit consensus treated as the reference norm.
  • LLM-based scorers.
  • Single-turn, so no pushback dynamics.

Relation to our finding

ELEPHANT shows a moral-consistency failure caused by social alignment: the same model tells both parties they are not at fault. Its verdict is set by the user's perspective, not by a stable moral position. This is a cross-context say–say inconsistency rather than our within-dialogue fold or our analysis→action gap. Still, it supports the idea that user-facing deference overrides stated moral judgments. Our agentic finding (a faithful analysis followed by a compromising action) extends this from verdict-flipping between users to a gap between what the model writes and what it does within a single episode.

Annotation

Broadens sycophancy to "social sycophancy" (preserving the user's face) and benchmarks 11 LLMs against human responses. Models validate 45–50 pp more than humans and affirm whichever side of an AITA conflict the user takes in 48% of paired cases. Preference datasets reward this behaviour. The main reference for implicit, ground-truth-free sycophancy and user-dependent moral verdicts.
