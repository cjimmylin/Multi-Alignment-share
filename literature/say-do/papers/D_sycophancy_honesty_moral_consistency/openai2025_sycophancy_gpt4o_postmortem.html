Say–do construct

  • No experimental construct. The incident is a deployment-scale case of sycophancy: an update made GPT-4o "overly flattering or agreeable."
  • In OpenAI's words it "skewed towards responses that were overly supportive but disingenuous" (2025a, as quoted by Willison). "Disingenuous" concedes a gap between the model's assessment and what it told users.

What happened (as quoted)

  • "[I]n this update, we focused too much on short-term feedback, and did not fully account for how users' interactions with ChatGPT evolve over time" (2025a).
  • The 25 April update combined candidate improvements, among them "an additional reward signal based on user feedback—thumbs-up and thumbs-down data from ChatGPT … in aggregate, these changes weakened the influence of our primary reward signal, which had been holding sycophancy in check. User feedback in particular can sometimes favor more agreeable responses" (2025b).
  • Memory: "in some cases, user memory contributes to exacerbating the effects of sycophancy, although we don't have evidence that it broadly increases it" (2025b).
  • Evaluation miss: "our offline evaluations—especially those testing behavior—generally looked good. Similarly, the A/B tests seemed to indicate that the small number of users who tried the model liked it … some expert testers had indicated that the model behavior 'felt' slightly off" (2025b).
  • Remedy: a system-prompt patch, then a full rollback. Commitments: "personality and other behavioral issues should be launch blocking," and proactive communication of updates (2025b).

Limitations

  • Self-reported and unquantified (no sycophancy rates published in the quoted text).
  • Accessed via secondary quotation.

Relation to our finding

The postmortem is real-world confirmation of the mechanism the academic literature proposes (Sharma et al. 2024): optimizing for short-term user approval weakens the counter-pressure against agreement. It is the obvious motivating citation for why folding under user pushback matters in deployment, and SYCON-Bench cites it the same way. It also shows that standard offline evaluations and A/B tests missed the problem, which argues for targeted multi-turn capitulation tests like ours.

Annotation
