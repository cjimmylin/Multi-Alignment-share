Fanous et al. (2025): SycEval — Evaluating LLM Sycophancy

  • Citation: Fanous, A., Goldberg, J., Agarwal, A. A., Lin, J., Zhou, A., Daneshjou, R., & Koyejo, S. (2025). SycEval: Evaluating LLM Sycophancy. AIES 2025. arXiv:2502.08177.
  • Venue: AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society (AIES) 2025, per the arXiv comment. Stanford.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08177
  • PDF downloaded: yes (fanous2025_syceval.pdf, 8 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: ad hoc (AMPS math, MedQuad; rebuttals written by Llama-3-8B; LLM-as-judge)

Say–do construct

  • "Say": the model's initial answer to a math or medical question.
  • "Do": its answer after rebuttals that escalate. A simple "you are wrong" comes first; later rebuttals add ethos, then a justification, then a fabricated citation with abstract.
  • Rebuttals are given in-context (after the answer) or pre-emptively (as a standalone prompt).
  • Changes are scored as progressive (moves to the correct answer) or regressive (moves to an incorrect one).
  • Note: the escalated rebuttals contain fabricated "evidence", so they are not content-free.

Method and models

  • ChatGPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet and Gemini-1.5-Pro.
  • 500 AMPS math and 500 MedQuad items, with human-validated LLM judging.

Key quantitative findings

  • Sycophancy in 58.19% of cases overall. Gemini 62.47%, Claude-Sonnet 57.44%, ChatGPT 56.71% (abstract, Results).
  • Progressive 43.52% vs regressive 14.66%. By model, regressive rates are Claude 18.31%, ChatGPT 14.40% and Gemini 9.25% (Results).
  • Pre-emptive rebuttals are more sycophantic than in-context ones (61.75% vs 56.52%; Z = 5.87, p < 0.001). On math, regressive sycophancy rises from 3.54% to 8.13% (abstract).
  • Simple rebuttals maximize progressive sycophancy. Citation-based rebuttals give the highest regressive rates (abstract).
  • Persistence: once sycophantic, models stay so through the rebuttal chain 78.5% of the time (95% CI 77.2–79.8%) (abstract).

Authors' explanation

Models defer to apparent authority and evidence. A fake citation is the most effective lever for pushing correct answers to wrong ones, which is risky in medicine.

Limitations

  • Three models and two domains.
  • Rebuttals written by a small LLM, with fabricated citations.
  • "Progressive sycophancy" mixes legitimate correction with deference.
  • LLM-as-judge.

Relation to our finding

SycEval's progressive/regressive split is the closest existing analogue to our two arms. Both directions of flip occur, but its stimuli mix authority, justification and fake evidence. Our design separates pushback without information (where any flip is a fold) from genuinely new information (where not updating is dig-in). The 14.66% regressive rate and the strong effect of citation-based rebuttals are useful benchmarks for how often correct positions are given up. Its 78.5% persistence result fits our pattern: once a model folds, it rarely recovers.

Annotation

Tests ChatGPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet and Gemini on math and medical questions under escalating rebuttals. Sycophancy appears in 58% of cases (14.7% regressive, i.e., moving to wrong answers), pre-emptive and citation-based rebuttals are most harmful, and sycophantic shifts persist 78.5% of the time. Useful for its progressive-vs-regressive framing. Its rebuttals blend authority with fabricated evidence.
