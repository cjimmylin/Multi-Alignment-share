Scherrer et al. (2023): Evaluating the Moral Beliefs Encoded in LLMs

  • Citation: Scherrer, N., Shi, C., Feder, A., & Blei, D. M. (2023). Evaluating the Moral Beliefs Encoded in LLMs. NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2307.14324.
  • Venue: NeurIPS 2023 (peer-reviewed). The arXiv record carries no venue note; acceptance is from the proceedings listing, so re-verify before citing.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14324 (dataset: MoralChoice)
  • PDF downloaded: yes (scherrer2023_moral_beliefs_llms.pdf, 32 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: theory-grounded/ad hoc. Scenarios are generated with LLM help and labelled with Gert's common-morality rules ("do not kill", "do not deceive"…).

Say–do construct

  • Not a say–do gap. It measures choice consistency of moral "beliefs" across semantically equivalent question forms.
  • The "belief" is the marginal action likelihood across six question forms (A/B, Repeat, Compare × two orderings).
  • Inconsistency is low question-form consistency (QF-C) and high entropy.

Method and models

  • MoralChoice: 687 low-ambiguity and 680 high-ambiguity scenarios, each with two actions and rule-violation labels.
  • 28 open and closed LLMs, from flan-t5-small to GPT-4 and Claude.
  • Statistical survey methodology: action likelihood, action entropy, QF-C, QF-E.

Key quantitative findings

  • In unambiguous scenarios most models choose the commonsense action. In ambiguous ones most express uncertainty (abstract, §4).
  • Some models are uncertain even on low-ambiguity items "because their responses are sensitive to the question-wording": small text-ada, babbage, curie, flan-t5-small and bloomz models (§4).
  • Some models show clear preferences in ambiguous cases. Closed-source models (gpt-4, claude-v1.3, claude-instant-v1.1, text-bison) agree strongly with each other (abstract, §4).

Authors' explanation

LLM survey responses must be modelled as distributions over question forms. Wording sensitivity is a major source of apparent moral uncertainty, and convergent closed-model preferences probably reflect similar alignment procedures.

Limitations

  • Scenarios partly LLM-generated.
  • Forced binary choices.
  • Measures stated choices, not behaviour.
  • Single-turn.

Relation to our finding

Scherrer et al. set the methodological baseline for treating LLM moral "beliefs" as probabilistic and wording-sensitive. Any claim that a model "abandoned" a moral or priority-faithful position must exceed this baseline of form-induced variability. Our capitulation and analysis→action measures are within-episode contrasts and so control for form effects in a way cross-item surveys cannot. MoralChoice is also the substrate for Huang et al.'s (2024) moral-persuasion study.

Annotation

Introduces MoralChoice (1,367 scenarios) and a statistical survey method for eliciting LLM moral beliefs across question forms, applied to 28 LLMs. Models pick commonsense actions in clear cases but are uncertain or wording-sensitive in ambiguous ones, and closed models converge. The methodological reference for consistency-aware measurement of LLM moral choices.
