Burns et al. (2023): Discovering Latent Knowledge in Language Models Without Supervision (CCS)

  • Citation: Burns, C., Ye, H., Klein, D., & Steinhardt, J. (2023). Discovering Latent Knowledge in Language Models Without Supervision. ICLR 2023. arXiv:2212.03827.
  • Venue: ICLR 2023 (peer-reviewed). Foundational for the honesty/latent-knowledge strand.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03827
  • PDF downloaded: yes (burns2023_latent_knowledge_ccs.pdf, 28 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: n/a (method over existing QA datasets)

Say–do construct

  • "Say": the model's zero-shot output to a yes/no question.
  • Latent "knowledge": a direction in activation space found without labels by Contrast-Consistent Search (CCS). CCS requires that a statement and its negation get opposite truth probabilities.
  • The key test is that models are prompted to generate incorrect answers: outputs degrade while latent knowledge stays put. The paper's framing is "what language models know, distinct from what they say."

Method and models

  • Six models: T5, UnifiedQA, T0, GPT-J, RoBERTa and DeBERTa.
  • Ten QA datasets.
  • Baselines: zero-shot and calibrated zero-shot; logistic regression as a supervised ceiling.

Key quantitative findings

  • CCS beats zero-shot accuracy by 4% on average across 6 models × 10 datasets (§3.2.1).
  • It halves the prompt sensitivity (standard deviation of accuracy) (§1).
  • With misleading prefixes, zero-shot accuracy drops by up to 9.5% while CCS accuracy holds (§3.2.2).

Authors' explanation

Imitation and human-feedback objectives can teach models to output what humans say or rate highly rather than the truth. An unsupervised consistency-based probe can recover truth-tracking internal representations that are less affected by such pressures.

Limitations

  • Pre-RLHF-era, mostly small models.
  • Binary questions only.
  • Later work questions whether CCS directions track truth rather than other salient features (not assessed here).
  • No conversational pressure.

Relation to our finding

CCS is the conceptual root of "latent knowledge survives output-level pressure." That is the representational analogue of our claim that capitulating models abandon an analysis they had grounds for. Burns et al.'s "pressure" is a misleading prompt prefix, not a pushback dialogue, and they measure internals, not behaviour. Our results are behavioural, and "belief" is operationalized as the model's own faithful first analysis. A natural extension would be to probe whether latent truth directions persist after a model folds.

Annotation

Introduces Contrast-Consistent Search, an unsupervised probe that finds truth-like directions in activations from logical consistency alone. It beats zero-shot accuracy by 4% and stays stable when prompts push models toward wrong outputs. Foundational for separating what models "know" from what they "say," and for the later honesty and lie-detection literature.
