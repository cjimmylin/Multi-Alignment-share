Jin et al. (2022): When to Make Exceptions — Exploring Language Models as Accounts of Human Moral Judgment (MoralExceptQA)

  • Citation: Jin, Z., Levine, S., Gonzalez, F., Kamal, O., Sap, M., Sachan, M., Mihalcea, R., Tenenbaum, J., & Schölkopf, B. (2022). When to Make Exceptions: Exploring Language Models as Accounts of Human Moral Judgment. NeurIPS 2022 (Oral). arXiv:2210.01478.
  • Venue: NeurIPS 2022, oral (peer-reviewed)
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01478 (code: github.com/feradauto/MoralCoT)
  • PDF downloaded: yes (jin2022_moralexceptqa.pdf, 29 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: theory-grounded (moral-psychology vignettes from contractualist "universalization" and rule-function studies; human judgment data)

Say–do construct

  • "Rule" (stated norm): e.g., no cutting in line, no damaging property, and a novel "no cannonballing in the pool" rule.
  • "Judgment": whether breaking the rule is OK in a specific vignette, compared with human permissibility judgments.
  • The construct is rule–exception flexibility, i.e., applying a stated principle to concrete cases. It is not a say–do gap or a pressure manipulation.

Method and models

  • MoralExceptQA challenge set: 148 vignettes in total. Cutting in line: 66. Property damage: 54. Cannonballing: 28. The rule should be broken in 39.19% of vignettes (Table 2).
  • Models: BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT, Delphi and Delphi++, GPT-3 and InstructGPT.
  • MoralCoT prompting breaks the decision into subquestions: Is the rule's purpose violated? Who is harmed? Who benefits?

Key quantitative findings

  • Most models score "not much better than the random baseline (around 50%)" F1 (§5).
  • MoralCoT reaches 64.47% F1, beating InstructGPT (53.94%) and Delphi++ (58.27%) by up to 6.2% F1 over the best prior model (Table 3).
  • Delphi and InstructGPT are highly conservative, almost always saying the rule must not be broken (conservativity 97.70% and 98.52%; Table 3).
  • For InstructGPT, the subquestion about the rule's purpose is hardest, while the literal-rule (loss) question is easiest (Table 4).

Authors' explanation

Human moral flexibility depends on reasoning about rule purpose and its costs and benefits. LLMs apply rules literally unless prompted to model that reasoning.

Limitations

  • Small challenge set.
  • Three norms.
  • Pre-ChatGPT models.
  • Predicts human judgments (descriptive) rather than normative correctness.

Relation to our finding

MoralExceptQA shows that principle-to-case application is a distinct competence: models can state a rule yet apply it rigidly or erratically to concrete cases. This is background for our agentic gap, where a model applies a stated priority correctly in its written analysis but not in its action. Jin et al. study judgment errors in applying rules, not divergence between the model's own judgment and its subsequent act. Our 13–28% compromising actions arise even when the analysis already got the application right.

Annotation

Introduces MoralExceptQA, a set of moral-psychology vignettes about when rules may permissibly be broken, and MoralCoT prompting, which reaches 64.5% F1 (+6.2 over the best baseline). Most 2022 LMs were near chance and overly rule-bound. A theory-grounded reference for the gap between stating rules and flexibly applying them to concrete cases.
