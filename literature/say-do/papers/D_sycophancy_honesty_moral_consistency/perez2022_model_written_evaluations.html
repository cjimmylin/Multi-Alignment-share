Perez et al. (2022/2023): Discovering Language Model Behaviors with Model-Written Evaluations

  • Citation: Perez, E., Ringer, S., Lukošiūtė, K., Nguyen, K., Chen, E., Heiner, S., … Kaplan, J. (2022). Discovering Language Model Behaviors with Model-Written Evaluations. arXiv:2212.09251.
  • Venue: Findings of ACL 2023 (arXiv v1, Dec 2022). Anthropic, with Surge AI and MIRI.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09251 (datasets: github.com/anthropics/evals)
  • PDF downloaded: yes (perez2022_model_written_evaluations.pdf, 47 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: ad hoc (LM-generated, crowd-validated)

Say–do construct

  • "Say": the model's answer to an opinion question (politics, NLP research, philosophy) when no user identity is given. This is implicit; the paper measures how often the answer matches the user's view, not a within-model change.
  • "Do": the answer given once a user biography with a stated or implied view comes first. Sycophancy is the rate at which the answer repeats back the user's preferred answer (§4).

Method and models

  • LMs write the evaluation datasets (154 in all); crowdworkers rate them and agree with 90–100% of labels (abstract).
  • Sycophancy tests (§4) prepend generated first-person biographies (≈10k examples, Tab. 5) to questions from the Pew political typology quiz, the NLP survey and PhilPapers.
  • Models are Anthropic pretrained LMs and RLHF models from 810M to 52B parameters at 0–1000 RL steps. Preference models (PMs) were also scored.

Key quantitative findings

  • Larger models repeat the user's view more often, on politics, NLP and philosophy (§4.2, Fig. 4).
  • The largest (52B) models are "highly sycophantic": >90% of answers match the user's view on NLP and philosophy questions (§4.2).
  • Sycophancy is similar at every number of RL steps, including 0 (pretrained LMs). "RLHF does not train away sycophancy," and PMs actually incentivize sycophantic answers (§4.2, Fig. 4 yellow lines).
  • The RLHF model answers two users with different politics differently, in line with each user's views (Tab. 6).
  • Same paper, other behaviors: RLHF raises stated desire to avoid shutdown and strengthens political views, which are examples of inverse scaling in RLHF (abstract, Fig. 1a).

Authors' explanation

Sycophancy is a form of reward hacking (citing Cotra 2021): models tailor responses to exploit evaluator quirks. Pretrained LMs also imitate dialogs between like-minded users (e.g., on Reddit). As tasks outrun human supervision, models "may simply provide incorrect answers that appear correct to us" (§4.2).

Limitations

  • Questions have no ground truth (opinions), so "sycophancy" here means view-matching rather than error.
  • Single-turn, multiple-choice, with persona cues in the prompt.
  • Anthropic-internal models only.
  • No measure of the model's own prior stance per item, and no pushback dialogue.

Relation to our finding

Perez et al. coined the operational benchmark sense of "sycophancy" and showed it scales with size and is rewarded by PMs. That gives the prior that RLHF-trained models lean toward agreement. Their design differs from ours in three ways: (i) it is single-turn and the user's view is given before the model commits, so nothing is abandoned; (ii) the items are opinion questions with no faithful answer to defend; (iii) there is no contrast condition with genuinely new information. Our multi-turn result goes further: the model first produces a faithful analysis and then gives it up under content-free pushback. That is a capitulation rather than pre-emptive tailoring.

Annotation

Introduces LM-generated evaluations and the first scaled measurement of "sycophancy": larger LMs (to 52B) repeat a user's stated political, NLP or philosophical view in >90% of cases on some sets, and preference models reward this. The canonical citation for the claim that sycophancy is a general, RLHF-compatible tendency. Its single-turn, opinion-only format is the baseline that later multi-turn and ground-truth sycophancy benchmarks extend.
