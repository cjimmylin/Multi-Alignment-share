Bonagiri et al. (2024): SaGE — Evaluating Moral Consistency in Large Language Models

  • Citation: Bonagiri, V. K., Vennam, S., Govil, P., Kumaraguru, P., & Gaur, M. (2024). SaGE: Evaluating Moral Consistency in Large Language Models. LREC-COLING 2024. arXiv:2402.13709.
  • Venue: LREC-COLING 2024 (peer-reviewed)
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13709 (code/data: github.com/vnnm404/SaGE)
  • PDF downloaded: yes (bonagiri2024_sage_moral_consistency.pdf, 13 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: crowdsourced-folk/ad hoc (questions from Moral Integrity Corpus; LLM paraphrases; LLM-generated Rules of Thumb)

Say–do construct

  • Consistency under paraphrase. For semantically equivalent moral questions, does the model follow the same underlying Rule of Thumb (RoT), an abstract principle extracted from each question–answer pair?
  • Semantic Graph Entropy (SaGE) measures how spread out the RoTs are across paraphrases. It is say–say consistency, with no pressure manipulation.

Method and models

  • Moral Consistency Corpus (MCC): 50K moral questions with LLM answers and RoTs; 10K sampled for experiments.
  • Paraphrases from Vicuna, filtered with ParaScore.
  • 11 LLMs: OPT 125m–13b, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Falcon-7B-instruct, Llama-2-7b and 13b chat, GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4.
  • Also applied to TruthfulQA and HellaSwag.

Key quantitative findings

  • No model exceeds a SaGE score of 0.681 (GPT-3.5 Turbo, on RoTs). GPT-4 scores 0.641 (Table 1).
  • Consistency does not depend on sampling temperature (§5.3, Fig. 3).
  • Human validation of RoT judgments: Krippendorff's α = 0.868.
  • "Task-accuracy and consistency are independent problems" (abstract).

Authors' explanation

Where moral questions lack ground truth, consistency is the reliability criterion. LLMs do not apply stable principles across paraphrases, and consistency is an intrinsic property separate from accuracy.

Limitations

  • RoTs are generated by an LLM.
  • Paraphrase quality depends on the generator.
  • Older models.
  • Single-turn.

Relation to our finding

SaGE quantifies moral inconsistency under meaning-preserving perturbation. That is the floor of instability any pressure-induced change must be judged against. Our scripted pushback is not a paraphrase: it holds content constant while adding social disagreement. Our finding is therefore about direction (systematic movement toward the user), not random principle drift. SaGE's "consistency ≠ accuracy" parallels MASK's "honesty ≠ accuracy" and our observation that faithful analysis does not guarantee faithful action.

Annotation

Proposes Semantic Graph Entropy, a measure of whether a model follows the same "rule of thumb" across paraphrased moral questions, and the 50K-question Moral Consistency Corpus. No tested LLM exceeds 0.681, and consistency is independent of task accuracy and temperature. A reference for paraphrase-level moral (in)consistency.
