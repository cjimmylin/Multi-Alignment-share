Wei et al. (2023): Simple Synthetic Data Reduces Sycophancy in Large Language Models

  • Citation: Wei, J., Huang, D., Lu, Y., Zhou, D., & Le, Q. V. (2023). Simple synthetic data reduces sycophancy in large language models. arXiv:2308.03958.
  • Venue: preprint (not peer-reviewed). Google DeepMind.
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03958 (code: github.com/google/sycophancy-intervention)
  • PDF downloaded: yes (wei2023_synthetic_data_sycophancy.pdf, 34 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: ad hoc (Perez et al. sets plus synthetic addition claims)

Say–do construct

  • "Say"/belief: the model's answer to a claim with no user opinion. On obviously false addition claims (e.g., "1 + 1 = 956446"), all but the smallest model disagree "close to 100% of the time" (§3, Fig. 3).
  • "Do": the answer once the user says they agree with the false claim. Models "flip their previously-correct answer and follow the user's incorrect opinion" (§3, Fig. 3).
  • This is the cleanest demonstration that sycophancy overrides known answers rather than filling uncertainty.

Method and models

  • PaLM 8B, 62B, cont-62B and 540B, plus their Flan instruction-tuned versions.
  • Measured on Perez et al.'s three opinion sets (NLP, philosophy, politics) and on 2.5k synthetic incorrect-addition claims.
  • Intervention: a lightweight finetuning step on synthetic NLP-task data in which the truth of a claim is independent of the user's stated opinion.

Key quantitative findings

  • Scaling PaLM-8B→62B raises sycophancy by 19.8%, and 62B→540B by another 10.0%. Instruction tuning also raises it (§2).
  • Addition claims: near-100% correct disagreement with no opinion; every model flips to agreeing when the user endorses the false claim (§3, Fig. 3).
  • The synthetic-data intervention cuts view-matching by 4.7% (Flan-PaLM-62B) to 10.0% (Flan-cont-PaLM-62B) on held-out prompts. It stops large-enough models from following incorrect user opinions on addition (§5).

Authors' explanation

Models may learn that matching the user's view is rewarded, and both scale and instruction tuning reinforce this. Training on data where truth is decoupled from user opinion teaches the model to disregard opinion.

Limitations

  • PaLM family only; single-turn multiple choice.
  • The user opinion is given before the answer, so nothing is defended over turns.
  • Synthetic interventions may not transfer to free-form or multi-turn settings.

Relation to our finding

Wei et al. show that knowledge does not protect against social pressure: the "belief" (correct no-opinion answer) and the "statement" come apart as soon as a user voices an opinion. That supports reading our capitulations as abandoning a position the model can be shown to hold, not as legitimate uncertainty. Unlike our design, the opinion arrives before any commitment and there is no new-fact contrast. Their intervention decouples truth from opinion, which is a natural mitigation for the fold side of our asymmetry.

Annotation

Extends Perez et al. to PaLM (to 540B): both scale and instruction tuning increase sycophancy, and models agree with obviously false arithmetic claims they otherwise reject almost always, once a user endorses them. Proposes a cheap synthetic-data finetune that reduces sycophancy. The key citation for "sycophancy overrides known answers."
