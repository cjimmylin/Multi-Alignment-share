Xu et al. (2024): The Earth is Flat because… — Investigating LLMs' Belief towards Misinformation via Persuasive Conversation

  • Citation: Xu, R., Lin, B. S., Yang, S., Zhang, T., Shi, W., Zhang, T., Fang, Z., Xu, W., & Qiu, H. (2024). The Earth is Flat because…: Investigating LLMs' Belief towards Misinformation via Persuasive Conversation. ACL 2024 (main). arXiv:2312.09085.
  • Venue: ACL 2024, main conference (peer-reviewed)
  • URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09085
  • PDF downloaded: yes (xu2024_earth_is_flat_persuasion.pdf, 45 pp.)
  • Instrument provenance: ad hoc (Farm dataset: BoolQ, NQ, TruthfulQA questions plus LLM-generated persuasive misinformation)

Say–do construct

  • "Belief": the model's answer to a factual question it initially answers correctly, with self-reported confidence (initial belief check).
  • "Do": its answer after up to four turns of persuasive misinformation (final belief check).
  • Misinformation strategies: repetition of the false claim, or rhetorical appeals (logical, credibility, emotional).
  • The appeals contain fabricated "evidence" (not genuinely new facts), and repetition contains none.

Method and models

  • Farm: 1,952 entries (questions from BoolQ, NQ1/NQ2 and TruthfulQA) paired with persuasive misinformation (§2; App. table of filtered counts).
  • Models: ChatGPT, GPT-4, Llama-2-7B-chat, Vicuna-v1.5-7B and 13B.
  • Metrics: misinformed rate (MR@n), accuracy (ACC@n), and confidence shifts.
  • Response types: rejection, sycophancy, uncertainty, acceptance, self-inconsistency.

Key quantitative findings

  • After one turn, belief alteration ranges from 4.1% to 63.4%; by turn four, 20.7% (GPT-4) to 78.2% (Llama-2-7B-chat) (§4.3, Finding I–II).
  • ChatGPT's beliefs are altered on 50.1% of Farm and GPT-4's on 20.7% (§1).
  • Repetition alone works: GPT-4's MR doubled after three extra turns of repeating the claim on NQ2 (Finding III). Rhetorical appeals beat repetition, and logical appeals are most effective (Findings IV–V).
  • Confidence in correct answers falls after misinformation. Some models show confidence rebounds consistent with a backfire effect (§4.3).
  • Better-informed models are more robust (Finding II).

Authors' explanation

LLMs show human-like susceptibility: repetition (an illusory-truth effect) and appeals shift "beliefs," and models with more knowledge resist better.

Limitations

  • Misinformation is LLM-generated.
  • Belief is operationalized by QA answers.
  • Older open models.
  • Sycophancy (outward agreement) is not cleanly separated from real belief change.

Relation to our finding

Xu et al. show multi-turn persuasion flips correct factual beliefs. Most relevant is that mere repetition, which adds no information, roughly doubles GPT-4's misinformed rate, close to our scripted content-free pushback. Their target is a fact rather than an analysis tied to a stated priority, and there is no arm testing acceptance of true new facts. Our asymmetry result complements theirs: the same pressure that dislodges correct positions is rarely matched by resistance to genuine corrections.

Annotation

Builds the Farm dataset and a multi-turn protocol in which persuasive misinformation is applied to facts LLMs initially know. Correct beliefs flip in 20.7% (GPT-4) to 78.2% (Llama-2-7B) of cases within four turns, and simple repetition doubles GPT-4's misinformed rate. A key ACL reference for multi-turn belief erosion without real new evidence.
