Say–do: AI research on what systems state versus what they do, 2021–2026

A collection of 105 papers (108 files including two OpenAI posts captured via a secondary site) on the gap between what language models and LLM agents say (stated values, written reasoning, their own judgement that an action is wrong, commitments to users) and what they do, gathered as background for the moral-psychology benchmark paper. Each paper is a PDF with a markdown summary beside it; a consolidated report reads across the whole collection.

Start with the report: reports/AI say do gap literature.md (about 9,800 words). It covers the five themes below, the benchmarks and methods, the main numbers, five open disagreements, and a positioning section for the paper (its nearest neighbours, where the 13–28 % rate sits among reported rates, claims to make and words to avoid, threats to validity and follow-up experiments). Its appendix lists every paper with its file base name.

Files are named <firstauthor><year>_<short_slug>.pdf with a matching .md. Each summary gives the full citation, venue (or "preprint, not peer-reviewed"), URL, what counts as "say" and as "do", method and models, key numbers with section or table references, the authors' explanation, limitations, how the paper relates to and differs from our findings, and a 2–4 sentence annotation for a bibliography.

Caveats for citing

  • Venues marked "re-verify" or "unverified" in the summaries and the report's appendix came from conference listings or arXiv comments rather than the version read; check them before citing.
  • The OpenAI GPT-4o sycophancy postmortem quotes (D_.../openai2025_*_VIA_WILLISON.html) are captures from Simon Willison's posts, because openai.com blocked automated access; check against the original pages.
  • Some numbers differ between arXiv versions (e.g., ODCV-Bench); cite the version the summary names.
  • Leads noted but not read (listed in the report) are not evidence until opened.
  • The PDFs are third-party papers kept here for private research use; they are not copied to the public share site.
